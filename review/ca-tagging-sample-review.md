# California taxonomy tagging: sample for review

50 bills from the 2025–26 session, tagged by the model from each bill's title and the Legislative Counsel's digest. Every tag carries a quoted sentence from the digest.
The sample deliberately over-represents guns, abortion, AI, data centers, housing, schools, immigration, autonomous vehicles and privacy, plus random others.

**How to review:** for each bill, mark each tag **OK**, **Wrong direction**, **Wrong topic**, or **Unsupported**, and add a note where useful.
Direction words: *expands / restricts* for access and rights sub-topics; *tightens / loosens* for regulation sub-topics; funding is a separate field.

Questions at the end of the file need your decision before a full run.

## Decisions needed before a full run

1. **Low-confidence tags.** About a fifth of tags are marked low confidence and often look like stretches (for example, a firearms-sentencing bill also tagged under firearms in schools). Drop low-confidence tags by default, or keep and label them?
2. **Too many tags per bill.** The model averages 2 tags per bill and up to 4. Should we cap at 3, or require medium confidence for any tag beyond the first?
3. **Enforcement and liability bills.** A bill that only says who gets the traffic citation (for example AB 2193 on autonomous vehicles) was tagged *restricts*. Is that right, or should rules that assign responsibility without limiting the activity be *neutral* or *mixed*?
4. **Study and assessment bills.** SB 1168 asks the PUC to *assess* data center rate structures and was tagged *tightens*. Should studies and assessments be *neutral* until they change something?
5. **Voluntary programs.** AB 1974 creates a voluntary firearm storage program and was tagged *tightens* on safe storage. Does a voluntary program count as tightening?
6. **The 'never unclear' problem.** The model now never answers *unclear*. I would add an explicit rule that thin or procedural digests must be *unclear* or *neutral*. Agree?
