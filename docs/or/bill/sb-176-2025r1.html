SB 176 (2025R1) · Relating to marijuana for medical use; prescribing an effective date.

Ends the medical marijuana grow registry, adds grow site administrator designations and retailer training, requires medical-grade CBD products, and adds cardholder protections.

What the bill does (OR measure summary (OLIS plain-language digest + catchline))

<b>Digest: The Act ends the OHA medical marijuana grow site registry system. The Act makes some medical marijuana growers get designated by the OLCC. The Act also says that some marijuana workers have to take a class on how to be responsible sellers. The Act tells all marijuana retailers they have to have at least some items for sale that have certain CBD strengths.</b> [<i>Digest: The Act says that some people who grow medical marijuana do not have to register with the OHA and that some others have to get a designation from the OLCC. The Act also protects some people who use medical marijuana from some kinds of unfair acts at work and in health care.</i>] [<i>Removes requirements that a registry identification cardholder who produces marijuana for personal medical use register with the Oregon Health Authority.</i>]<b> Removes the requirement that the Oregon Health Authority establish a marijuana grow site registration system. Requires the authority to establish a data collection system related to the medical use of marijuana.</b> Requires a person responsible for a marijuana grow site that produces marijuana for medical use for three or more registry identification cardholders to apply for a <b>grow site administrator</b> designation from the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission. [<i>Directs the authority to issue electronic registration cards. Requires a marijuana retailer to offer for sale medical grade cannabinoid items that contain not more than 20 percent total THC.</i>] Creates health care [<i>and employment protections</i>] for a person who is a registry identification cardholder.<b> Directs the Department of Revenue to distribute moneys to the authority for the purposes of paying specified costs related to the administration of the medical marijuana program. Becomes operative on January 1, 2026. <b>Directs the authority to issue electronic registry identification cards. Becomes operative on January 1, 2027. <b>Requires an individual who performs work for or on behalf of a marijuana retailer licensed by the commission to complete a responsible retailer certification training program. Requires marijuana retailers to carry marijuana items with specified cannabidiol potencies. Becomes operative on January 1, 2026.</b> Takes effect on the 91st day following adjournment sine die.
