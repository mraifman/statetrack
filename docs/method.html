How it works

What is labelled

Each California bill gets one or more tags. A tag has a sub-topic from a fixed two-level list (16 topics, 115 sub-topics), a direction on that sub-topic, a mechanism (for example a ban, a licensing rule, a tax incentive, a study) and a funding note (increases, decreases, none).

Direction

Direction describes what the bill does, not whether that is good or bad. For rights and access sub-topics it is expands or restricts. For regulation sub-topics it is tightens or loosens. Funding-only sub-topics have no direction. Studies, assessments, voluntary programs and rules that only assign responsibility are labelled neutral.

The restrictive-to-permissive meter

Topic and sub-topic pages place each category on one line from restrictive to permissive. Each directional tag is scored: strongly restricts or tightens −2, restricts or tightens −1, mixed 0, expands or loosens +1, strongly expands or loosens +2. The marker sits at the average. Neutral, unclear and funding-only tags are left out, and a hollow marker means fewer than 8 directional tags. The average hides disagreement inside a topic: a marker near the middle can mean balanced bills or a mix of very restrictive and very permissive ones, so open the topic to see the bills.

Evidence

Every direction call must quote a sentence from the Legislative Counsel’s digest of the bill. If the quoted sentence is not in the digest, the call is thrown away and shown as unclear.

Who does the labelling

An AI model (claude-haiku-5-5) reads each bill’s title and digest and applies the list above. It is fast and cheap but not perfect: in a 50-bill test it agreed with a larger model on about 86% of direction calls. Bills without a digest are tagged from the title alone and usually come out unclear on direction. The model declined to process one bill (a biosecurity bill) and it is shown untagged. Treat labels as a guide to read the bill, not a substitute.

Legislators

Legislator pages count what each member authored or co-authored and how they voted on the floor, by sub-topic and direction, with every bill linked. Committee votes are not yet matched to members. A co-author can sign on for many reasons, so counts are evidence, not a rating, and the site computes no overall score.
