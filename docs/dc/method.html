How it works

The benchmark: current law

Every direction is measured against the status quo: current DC law, as the legislation summary describes it. Permissive means the bill moves the law toward more permission, access or fewer rules. Restrictive means it moves toward less permission or more rules. A bill that continues, studies or clarifies current law is neutral and is left out of the averages.

What is labelled

Each DC bill gets one or more tags. A tag has a sub-topic from a fixed two-level list (16 topics, 101 sub-topics, version 1.1), a direction against the status quo, a mechanism (a ban, a licensing rule, a tax incentive, a study) and a funding note (increases, decreases, none). Ceremonial measures (naming, honoring) are tagged as “not a policy topic” and left out of the charts.

Direction

Every bill is labelled Restrictive, Permissive, Mixed or Neutral on each sub-topic it touches, with “Strongly” where the change is large. The AI model’s own wording differs by sub-topic (expands or restricts for access and rights, tightens or loosens for regulation); the site maps both to Permissive and Restrictive, and the original word appears as a tooltip. Funding-only sub-topics have no direction. Studies, assessments, voluntary programs and rules that only assign responsibility are Neutral. Direction describes what a bill does, not whether that is good or bad.

The restrictive-to-permissive meter

Each directional tag is scored: Strongly Restrictive −2, Restrictive −1, Mixed 0, Permissive +1, Strongly Permissive +2. A marker sits at the average. Neutral, unclear and funding-only tags are left out, and a hollow marker means fewer than 8 directional tags. The average hides disagreement inside a topic, so topic pages show access and regulation separately.

Show Bills that Died

By default the site leaves out bills whose official status is “Died”, “Failed Passage in Committee” or “Vetoed” from every count, meter and list. Check Show Bills that Died in the header to include them. A bill still shown as “in committee” after the session ended cannot pass, but it is not hidden unless the record marks it as died.

Legislator tags

On each legislator’s page, every issue gets a Restrictive, Permissive or Balanced tag from the bills they authored or co-authored. Lead authors count 3×, principal co-authors 2× and co-authors 1×. A tag needs at least 3 directional bills; a weighted average of −0.25 or lower is Restrictive and +0.25 or higher is Permissive. The tag describes the bills, not the person’s views, and co-authors sometimes sign on without strong support. The dark tick on each meter marks the average of all DC bills on that topic.

Issues in the news

The issues on the overview were picked from news coverage as of October 2026. Each issue page lists its sources and states exactly which bills it includes (by tag and by wording in the title or digest). Keyword matching can include a bill that only mentions the subject in passing.

Bill status

Statuses come from the Legislature’s record. Became Law means the bill was chaptered: the Mayor signed it, or it became law without a signature, and it has a chapter number. Vetoed bills were returned by the Mayor. Died and Failed Passage in Committee bills did not advance.

Autonomous vehicles and driver assistance

These are separate sub-topics. Autonomous vehicles covers driverless or automated driving systems that operate without a human driver continuously in control. Driver-assistance systems (ADAS) covers partially automated features where a human must stay in control, such as lane-keeping and adaptive cruise control. A drunk-driving or other traffic offense by a human using these features is filed under Traffic safety unless the bill regulates the feature itself.

Reading legislator pages

Click a topic to see that legislator’s sub-topics, each with its own score, and click a sub-topic to see the bills behind it, with their role and direction. The label on a topic is a summary of those sub-topic scores.

Evidence

Every direction call must quote a sentence from the bill’s title or the legislation summary. If the quoted sentence is not in the digest, the call is thrown away and shown as unclear.

Who does the labelling

An AI model (claude-haiku-5-5) reads each bill’s title and digest and applies the list above. It is fast and cheap but not perfect: in a 50-bill test it agreed with a larger model on about 86% of direction calls. Bills without a digest are tagged from the title alone and usually come out unclear on direction. The model declined to process one bill (a biosecurity bill) and it is shown untagged. Labels are a guide to read the bill, not a substitute.

Legislators

Legislator pages count what each member authored or co-authored and how they voted on the floor, with every bill linked. Committee votes are not yet matched to members.
