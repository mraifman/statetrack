HB 2354 · Common interest communities

Exempts small middle-housing common interest communities from most WUCIOA rules and raises the association audit threshold from $50,000 to $100,000.

What the bill does (WA Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

A common interest community qualifies for the small community exemption if the community's amended declaration—and not just the original declaration—contains the requisite language regarding maximum assessments. Small common interest communities that consist only of middle housing are exempt from most WUCIOA provisions. Specifically, if a plat or miscellaneous community consists of no more than six middle housing units and the average annual assessment of all middle housing units does not exceed $1,000, the community is subject only to the following provisions of the WUCIOA: the requirement of a separate title for each unit as a separate parcel of real estate and a separate tax assessment based on the value of that unit together with its interest in the common elements; the prohibition of discriminatory application of building, fire, health, or safety codes and regulations by local governments against the community property based only on the fact that the property is owned as a common interest community; and general provisions that apply when a unit in a common interest community is taken or partially taken by eminent domain. All middle housing common interest communities are exempt from the requirement to prepare and annually update a reserve study if a community does not, and will not in the future, require the construction, operation, and maintenance of any reserve component to manage wastewater and protect health and safety, as well as ground and service waters. The governing documents of a common interest community may not vary from the WUCIOA on the subject of financial responsibility for electric vehicle charging stations and heat pumps. Only the owner of the charging station or heat pump exclusively serving the owner's unit bears that financial responsibility. The threshold that triggers the requirement for an association to be annually audited by a certified public accountant and that allows unit owners to waive the annual audit is increased from $50,000 to $100,000.
