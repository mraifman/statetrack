SB 6246 · Emissions/trade-exposed

Requires Ecology to recommend 2035-2050 no-cost allowance schedules for trade-exposed industries and removes the 2027 legislative deadline to act.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

North American Industry Classification System Codes. The NAICS codes applying to EITEs in the Program are those classifications in existence as of January 1, 2026. Recommendations to the Legislature. By December 1, 2026, Ecology must provide recommendations to the Legislature regarding the schedule of allowances provided to EITEs between 2035-2050. The recommendations in the report must identify: a proposed method for making annual reductions to EITE allowance allocation that would mitigate the risk of leakage and ensure the total no-cost allowances allocated align with the annual allowance budgets and which are consistent with the state GHG emissions limits, including the percentage reductions in EITE allowance allocation that would be applied each year between 2035-2050; proposed criteria and methods to make adjustments to no-cost allowances allocated to EITEs to address significant changes in leakage risk and to achieve the purposes of the Program; the proposed design of an allowance allocation policy or method that would require a portion of no-cost allowances to be consigned to auction and for the proceeds to be invested in projects or programs to reduce GHG emissions at the EITEs from which they were consigned, including the percentage of allowances to be consigned and methods for the distribution and use of consigned funds at each EITE facility; additional state policies or strategies that may be necessary to support decarbonization of EITEs to achieve the state GHG emissions limits, including consideration of factors that may affect deployment, such as technical and economic feasibility and infeasibility; and provisions of the CCA or other state laws needing to be amended to implement the recommendations above. In developing the recommendations, Ecology must consider input received from representatives of EITEs, other covered entities, environmental advocates, overburdened communities, tribes, other experts, and the public. The recommendations should consider the: anticipated demand for allowances from EITE facilities and other covered entities through 2050; potential for deployment of technologies and strategies to reduce emissions at EITE facilities and other facility- or industry-specific factors, including factors that may affect deployment, such as technical and economic feasibility and infeasibility; potential impacts of implementing the recommendations on overburdened communities and vulnerable populations; and interactions with other state policies and programs designed to reduce GHG emissions and achieve state GHG emissions limits. In addition to the recommendations, Ecology may include information on additional state policies or strategies that incentivize EITE facilities to use lower-carbon raw materials, recycled materials, or material substitution, to reduce the emissions attributable to the manufacture of an individual good or goods in the state. If the Legislature does not adopt a schedule of allowances provided to EITEs between 2035-2050, EITE facilities must continue receiving no-cost allowances as provided in the third compliance period until a schedule is adopted by the Legislature. The bill removes the December 1, 2027, deadline in current law, by which the Legislature must act. Emissions-Intensive, Trade-Exposed Facilities Reporting Requirements.
