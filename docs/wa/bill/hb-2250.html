What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

Eligibility for hospital charity care is limited to only Washington residents. The term "resident" includes a person, including an emancipated person under the age of 18 and a married person under the age of 18 who is capable of indicating intent, who currently lives in Washington and: intends to reside in Washington indefinitely; entered the state looking for a job; or entered the state with a job commitment. A child under the age of 18 may be considered a resident if they live in Washington or the child's parent or caretaker meets the criteria for being a resident. There is no minimum time period for living in Washington prior to meeting the criteria for residency. Residents may leave Washington for over a month and not lose their residency status if they: (1) intend to return to Washington once the reason for leaving is complete and this intent can be verified and (2) have not been determined eligible for Medicaid or state-funded health care coverage in another state, except for incidental or emergency health care. While a person who enters Washington for health care is not a resident and is not eligible for charity care, a person receiving emergency care under the federal Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act or the person's guarantor will be considered a resident. A person who receives a state supplemental payment is considered a resident of the state making the payment. A person who receives federal payments for foster care or adoption assistance is considered a resident of the state in which the person physically resides. A person's immigrant status may not be considered when determining a person's residency.
