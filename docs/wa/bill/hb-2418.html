HB 2418 · Permit review

Sets strict permit review deadlines and fee refunds for residential project permits, and limits completeness checks to procedural review.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

A permit issued based on compliance with state and local building codes is not a project permit. Nor is a special purpose district's or public utility district's determination of service available, system capacity, infrastructure requirements, or review of proposed ware, sewer, or stormwater civil plans. Determination of Completeness . A determination of completeness must be based on whether an application is procedurally complete. A determination of completeness is not a substantive review and cannot be conditioned on the adequacy, accuracy, or sufficiency of the information submitted. Permit Review Deadlines . Periods during which an applicant has not paid an applicable fee or posted a required notice, periods in which a draft of a final environmental impact statement is being prepared following a determination of significance under SEPA, and periods between the issuance of a final decision and the expiration of the applicable administrative review period are not included when determining the number of days a project permit application has been in review with a local government. Nor are periods in which a local government has completed all possible work on the application but is waiting on necessary action from another government entity. Any government entity other than a local government, special purpose district, or public utility district that imposes a fee on an applicant for the review of a project permit application related to a residential project must complete its review within the project permit review timelines applicable to a local government. The time period that a government entity has taken to conduct its review of the application is exclusive of the time period that the local government or another public agency has taken to review the application. If the review is not completed within the applicable timeframe, then the government entity must refund 20 percent of the permit review fee that it collected. If a government entity does not impose a fee for its review, then no refund is required. An applicant may, in writing, waive the permitting review deadlines applicable to a government entity's review of a project permit application. If an applicant does so, then provisions related to project permit review deadlines and to a potential refund do not apply to the application. Similarly, if an applicant agrees to the extension of the deadlines for a county or city to issue a decision on a project permit application, then the deadlines and refund provisions do not apply to that application. An applicant may agree to waive or extend the deadlines for a government entity or a local government at any time prior to the denial or the application or the expiration of the deadlines. Special purpose districts and public utility districts (districts) have different permitting deadlines for project permit applications related to residential projects than do local government or other government entities. For a district, the time for conducting a review of an application begins after it receives a complete application referral from the local government. A referral is complete if it provides civil plans and related materials that are based on the district's adopted engineering and design standards, and all materials required to constitute a complete application under the district's procedures are included.
