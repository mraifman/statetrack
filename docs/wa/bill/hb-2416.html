HB 2416 · Waste to energy facilities

Gives pre-1992 waste-to-energy facilities free emissions allowances through 2030, with banking and conditions, and requires a 2030 emissions and waste reduction plan.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

For emissions through 2030, Ecology must allocate no-cost allowances to a WTE facility constructed prior to 1992 that becomes a CCA covered entity at the beginning of the second compliance period, if the facility is operated in compliance with federal laws and regulations and meets state air quality standards. Relative to the facility's baseline average annual GHG emissions from 2021 through 2025, Ecology must allocate no cost allowance equal to 100 percent of baseline emissions for emissions year 2027 and 2028, 93 percent of baseline emissions for emissions year 2029, and 86 percent of baseline emissions for emissions year 2030. Forty percent of the allowances allocated to the WTE facility must be consigned to auction, with proceeds to be used, with Ecology approval, for investments in projects or programs that reduce GHG emissions associated with the WTE facility. The owner or operator of a WTE facility must submit a proposal to Ecology of how the investments in projects or programs will reduce GHG emissions before expending proceeds from consigned allowances. Ecology must review this proposal within 90 days, and the WTE facility owner or operator must address Ecology's comments, gain Ecology approval of its proposal, and take reasonable steps towards implementation of the proposal. If a WTE facility’s GHG emissions exceed the no-cost allowances allocated to the facility for 2027 through 2030, the WTE facility owner or operator must acquire additional compliance instruments equal to its compliance obligation. The WTE facility may bank unused allowances. Ecology must withhold or withdraw the relevant share of no-cost allowances to the WTE facility if the WTE facility ceases production and closes, with the withheld or withdrawn allowances being transferred to the CCA’s Emissions Containment Reserve. If the WTE facility curtails production, it may retain but not trade allowances during the period of curtailment. Ecology may not allocate no-cost allowances to electric utilities for GHG emissions associated with electricity produced by a WTE facility for which the WTE facility has a CCA compliance obligation. By December 1, 2030, the owner or operator of the WTE facility constructed prior to 1992 must provide a two-part plan to Ecology and the Department of Commerce. One portion of the plan is a greenhouse gas emission reduction plan that must outline how the facility will achieve emissions consistent with the state emissions limits for 2040 and 2050 through any combination of GHG emission reduction strategies. The other portion of the plan is a waste reduction and material recovery plan that must be consistent with the state's waste management hierarchy, take into consideration the organic material management policies that apply to local governments and businesses and the expected impacts of the 2025 Recycling Reform Act, and align with the county's local solid waste plan. Ecology may provide technical assistance and guidance for the development and implementation of the plan. The owner or operator of the WTE facility must consider social, environmental, and health factors in overburdened communities and vulnerable populations, and consult with local municipally created sustainability advisory groups. The facility owner or operator must submit the plan for review by Ecology in consultation with the Department of Commerce, and must address comments received from the agencies within 120 days.
