What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

An electric utility may satisfy its 2030 and 2045 CETA obligations by using electricity from certain natural gas systems operated with carbon capture and utilization, mineralization, or sequestration (CCUS) technology. The CCUS technology must comply with all local, state, and federal laws and have a capture design capacity of at least 75 percent of the baseline carbon dioxide (CO2) production of the applicable natural gas system. The method for calculating the baseline CO2 production of a natural gas system depends on the date that the gas system was originally placed in service: If the gas system was originally placed into service within a one-year period prior to the beginning of construction of the CCUS system, or the gas system had not yet been placed into service as of the beginning of construction of the CCUS system, the baseline CO2 production is the designed annual CO2 production, by mass, as determined based on an assumed 60 percent capacity factor. If the gas system was originally placed in service between one year and three years prior to the beginning of construction of the CCUS system, the baseline CO2 production is the average annual CO2 production by mass during the period between when the natural gas system was placed in service and when the CCUS construction began. If the gas system was originally placed in service more than three years prior to the beginning of construction of the CCUS system, the baseline CO2 production is the average of the three-year period with the highest annual CO2 production during the 12-year period before the beginning of construction of the CCUs system. An electric utility that has not achieved its 2030 CETA obligations is in compliance with CETA if it demonstrates that it has maximized investments in electricity from natural gas systems operated with CCUS, in addition to maximizing investments in electricity from renewable resources and non-emitting electric generation. Natural gas systems operated with CCUS are given treatment equivalent to renewable resources or non-emitting generation for other purposes under CETA, including for CEIP purposes, and for petitions by an investor-owned utility to the UTC for a declaratory order regarding the compliance of a proposed project with 2030 and 2045 CETA obligations.
