SB 5122 · Uniform antitrust premerger

Requires merging parties with Washington ties to file federal HSR premerger forms with the Attorney General, with confidentiality protections and penalties up to $10,000 per day.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Premerger Notification Requirements . A person filing a premerger notification with the United States pursuant to the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust Improvements Act must also make a contemporaneous filing of the HSR form with the Washington Attorney General if: (1) the person has its principal place of business in Washington; (2) the person or a person it controls directly or indirectly had annual net sales in Washington of goods or services involved in the transaction of at least 20 percent of the HSR filing threshold; or (3) the person is a health care provider or provider organization that conducts business in Washington. When the filer has its principal place of business in Washington, it must also file with the Attorney General a complete electronic copy of the documentary material filed with the federal HSR form. At the request of the Attorney General, when the filer had annual net sales in excess of the 20 percent threshold or the filer is a health care provider or provider organization, it must also file with the Attorney General a complete electronic copy of the documentary material filed with the federal HSR form. The Attorney General may not charge a fee in connection with the filing of the HSR form or related documentary material. Confidentiality Requirements . The Attorney General may not make public or disclose an HSR form or related documentary material filed under the Notification Act or provided by the attorney general of another state, the fact that such form or material was filed or provided, or the fact of the proposed merger. These forms, documents, and information are exempt from public inspection and copying under the Public Records Act. However, these forms, documents, and information may be disclosed in an administrative proceeding or judicial action if relevant and subject to a protective order. The Attorney General is not precluded from sharing information with the Federal Trade Commission, United States Department of Justice, a successor agency, or the attorney general of another state subject to certain requirements. Reciprocity . The Attorney General may disclose an HSR form and related documentary materials to the attorney general of another state that has enacted the Notification Act or a substantially similar act with confidentiality provisions that are at least as protective as the Notification Act's provisions. Before making such disclosure, the Attorney General must provide at least two business days of advance notice to the filer. Civil Penalty . The Attorney General may seek imposition of a civil penalty of not more than $10,000 per day of noncompliance for violations of the Notification Act's requirements, subject to procedural requirements. Construction . In applying and construing the Notification Act, courts are directed to consider the promotion of uniformity of the law among enacting jurisdictions. Prospective Application . The requirements of the Notification Act apply only to premerger notifications filed on or after its effective date. Health Care Transactions . A health care provider or provider organization that provides a copy of an HSR form to the Attorney General pursuant to the Notification Act is deemed to have satisfied the state law notice requirements for qualifying material change transactions among certain health care entities.
