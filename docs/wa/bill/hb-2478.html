HB 2478 · Wage enforcement discretion

Lets the Labor Department investigate wage complaints with set deadlines, tolls wage claim limitations, seeks penalties for willful violations, and requires a public complaint-priority process.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The Department is given discretion to investigate wage complaints under the Wage Payment Act. If the Department accepts a wage complaint, it must issue a citation and notice of assessment or a determination of compliance within 60 days of the complaint being accepted, rather than received. The statute of limitations for a civil action for an employee's wage complaint is tolled once the complaint is filed with the Department and ends when the Department notifies the employee the wage complaint is not accepted. When the Department initiates an investigation on its own and orders the employer to pay amounts owed in an administrative order, the Department may also assess penalties for willful violations under the WPA. Language is added making it explicit that the Department has authority to order payment of amounts owed, including interest, and to take assignment of claims under the WPA, the Minimum Wage Act, and the prevailing wage requirements. The Department must establish a written process to prioritize wage complaints and make its enforcement priorities publicly available. Substitute Bill Compared to Original Bill: The substitute adds the provisions requiring the Department to create a process to prioritize wage complaints.
