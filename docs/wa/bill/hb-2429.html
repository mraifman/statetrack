HB 2429 · Youth behavioral health

Extends the Children and Youth Behavioral Health Work Group to 2031, creates a Governor's leadership council, and requires alignment with the Washington Thriving Strategic Plan.

What the bill does (WA Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Activities of the Governor . The Office of the Governor, to the extent possible with sufficient existing resources, must support and facilitate coordination between various state agencies to follow the framework of the Washington Thriving Strategic Plan (Strategic Plan). Subject to the availability of sufficient private or other non-state funding, the Office of the Governor is required to maintain executive coordination for the children and youth system of care for the purpose of: monitoring the implementation of the Strategic Plan; building the infrastructure described in the Strategic Plan; coordinating private funding used to support the Strategic Plan; and monitoring the equity impacts, in consultation with the Washington State Office of Equity, of implementation of the Strategic Plan. The Governor must establish a leadership council to address children and youth health and wellness issues and coordinate efforts to implement the Strategic Plan. The leadership council must, at a minimum, consist of: representatives from state agencies involved in children and youth behavioral health or wellness activities; individuals with lived experience caring for individuals with behavioral health issues experienced as a child or youth; one member from the Children and Youth Behavioral Health Work Group (Work Group); and two representatives of federally recognized Indian tribes whose traditional lands and territories included parts of Washington, nominated by the Governor's Indian Health Advisory Council. The Office of the Governor is authorized to accept private or other nonstate funding to support the Strategic Plan. Children and Youth Behavioral Health Work Group . The Work Group must use the Strategic Plan to guide the priorities and efforts of the Work Group. The Work Group is extended two years, until December 30, 2031, (instead of expiring on December 30, 2029). The membership of the Work Group is modified to replace the participation by a representative of tribal governments with a requirement for the Governor to appoint two representatives of federally recognized Indian tribes whose traditional lands and territories included parts of Washington, nominated by the Governor's Indian Health Advisory Council. Alignment with the Washington Thriving Strategic Plan . State, tribal, and local government entities and nonprofit organizations involved in children and youth behavioral health activities must align with the Strategic Plan, and these activities must be implemented in an equitable manner.
