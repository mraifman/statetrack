What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

The WDFW must designate an ungulate population as "at-risk" and initiate predator mitigation within 60 days if any population within the federal delisting area experiences a reduction of 25 percent or more below its 10-year rolling average. "Ungulate" means mule deer, white-tailed deer, elk, moose, mountain goat, mountain caribou, and bighorn sheep. "Predator" includes bears, cougars, and wolves. If an ungulate population is designated at-risk, the WDFW must implement predator mitigation actions, including: seasonal or geographic predator reduction around sensitive ranges; translocation of gray wolves within Washington; and targeted removal or nonlethal mitigation. The WDFW must also monitor ungulates to determine population increases over time. Mitigation actions must continue until mule deer and white-tailed deer populations meet or exceed 2004 harvest levels for two consecutive years and exceed their 10-year rolling average. The WDFW must conduct and resume annual population counts of white-tailed deer in partnership with sportsmen. Survey methods must be collaboratively designed and publicly transparent. A report of the population counts and the annual deer season harvest reports must be published annually by March 31 or two weeks before the current seasons can be set. The WDFW must report ungulate population trends, at-risk designations, mitigation actions, and progress toward 2004 population benchmarks annually to the Legislature by March 31.
