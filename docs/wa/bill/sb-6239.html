SB 6239 · State tort claims

Creates a claims commission in the Office of Administrative Hearings to hear tort claims against the state, with tolling and a $25 filing fee.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Claims Commission. A claims commission is established within the office of administrative hearings for the purpose of adjudicating claims against the state and its political subdivisions, or against their officers, employees, or volunteers, acting in such capacity, for damages arising out of tortious conduct. Claims for personal injury must be brought in the commission before filing suit where the claim occurred ten or more years before the filing of the claim or when the claimant has reached the age of 18, whichever is later. Claims for personal injury may be brought in the commission where the claim injury occurred fewer than ten years before filing or when the claimant has reached the age of 18, whichever is later. The Office of Administrative Hearings shall administer and provide staff support to the claims commission. Claims shall be heard and determined using the Administrative Procedure Act. Members of the claims commission shall be appointed by the Governor and shall consist of: a representative of the Office of the Attorney General; a victim advocate; a mental health professional; a representative of the Washington State Association for Justice; a single representative for all state agencies; a single representative for all counties; a single representative for all school districts; a representative of the Office of Financial Management, who may assign a state actuary; and a representative from the Department of Enterprise Services. The duties of the members of the claims commission include, but are not limited to: appointing neutral hearing officers; maintaining a registry of neutral hearing officers; adopting rules governing the procedural conduct of hearings, consistent with the Administrative Procedure Act; and delivering annual reports on the commission's work to the appropriate committees of the Legislature by December 15th of each year. Neutral Hearings Officer. A neutral hearing officer may set and conduct hearings, determine credibility, and issue written decisions and awards. The neutral hearing officer shall be a retired Washington superior court judge, retired appellate court judge, or former administrative law judge with substantial experience adjudicating tort claims. The neutral hearing officer shall be trained in trauma-informed practices, including recognizing the impacts of trauma on participants and conducting proceedings in a manner that promotes fairness, safety, and respect. Claims. Before filing a claim with the claims commission, a claimant must comply with the presentment and filing requirements outlined in law for state and local agencies. The filing of a claim with the commission shall toll the applicable statute of limitations. Neutral hearing officers shall be assigned on a rotating or random basis, subject to disqualification for cause. Each party shall be entitled to one peremptory strike of an assigned neutral hearing officer, exercised within ten days of assignment. At the time of filing, a claimant shall select either the expedited examiner track or the hearing track. Expedited Examiner Track. Claimants electing to proceed on the expedited examiner track shall submit with their claim a statement signed under penalty of perjury identifying the location and approximate dates of abuse, the name of the perpetrator or perpetrators, if known, witnesses, if any, and claimed damages as well as any information in the claimant's possession or control substantiating the claim. There is a filing fee of $25
