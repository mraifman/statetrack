HB 2152 · Cannabis/health facilities

Requires hospitals, nursing homes and hospice facilities to allow terminal patients to use medical cannabis on premises under facility policies.

What the bill does (WA Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Hospitals, nursing homes, and hospice care centers (collectively "facilities") must allow the medical use of cannabis by qualifying patients with a terminal condition while the patient is on the facility's premises. Nursing homes operated by a residential habilitation center are exempt from the medical use of cannabis requirements. As applied to hospitals, the requirement does not apply to emergency departments, patients receiving emergency care, or any patient of the hospital who has not been admitted to the hospital to receive inpatient services. A "terminal condition" is defined as an incurable and irreversible condition that, within reasonable medical judgment, will cause death within a reasonable period of time. Each facility must adopt a policy related to the medical use of cannabis on its premises. The policies must: prohibit smoking or vaping as a method of use; require the patient's medical use of cannabis to be included in the patient's medical records; require patients with a terminal condition to provide a copy of their authorization; require the patient or the patient's designated provider to be responsible for acquiring, retrieving, administering, and removing medical cannabis; require medical cannabis to be stored securely in a locked container in the patient's room, a designated location, or with the patient's designated provider; prohibit health care professionals and facility staff from administering medical cannabis or retrieving medical cannabis from storage; prohibit sharing medical cannabis between patients and visitors; and require the patient or the patient's designated provider to remove remaining cannabis and direct the facility to dispose of any remaining medical cannabis in accordance with procedures governing medical waste. Compliance with the medical use of cannabis requirements may not be a condition for a facility's licensure. Facilities may suspend compliance with the medical use of cannabis requirements if the federal government initiates an enforcement action against a hospital related to its compliance with the medical use of cannabis requirements or issues a rule or other notification that expressly prohibits the medical use of cannabis in health care facilities. The status of cannabis as a Schedule I controlled substance under federal law does not allow a facility to prohibit the medical use of cannabis on its premises. The act is named "Ryan's Law."
