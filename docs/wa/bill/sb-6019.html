What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Home Care Agency Vendor Rate . The home care agency vendor rate is the sum of the portion of the vendor rate dedicated to the wages, benefits, and required employer contributions of premiums for home care agency workers who provide direct care and the home care agency administrative rate. For fiscal year 2027, the Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS) shall determine, in its sole discretion, the portion of the total vendor rate dedicated to wages, benefits, and required employer contributions for home care agency workers who provide direct care. Beginning in fiscal year 2028, this portion of the rate is equal to the previous fiscal year plus the incremental changes calculated using the current process. The portion of the vendor rate calculated for training may only be used for training, testing, and certification costs, as well as costs associated with the administration of training, testing, and certification scheduling and compliance for home care agency workers who provide direct care. The board must review the projection from the DSHS of the portion of the vendor rate dedicated to the wages and benefits for home care agency workers who provide direct care and the required employer contributions or premiums. Verification of Expended Funds . Beginning July 1, 2027, the DSHS or its designee must verify that the home care agencies have expended funds in accordance with the wages and benefits, health care rate, and training rate requirements. Each home care agency must submit one of the following to assist the DSHS in this verification process: an independent third-party audit conducted by a qualified, accredited firm to validate compliance with requirements; or a written attestation from the labor organization that is the exclusive bargaining representative of the home care workers who provide direct care that are employed by the agency, confirming that the home care agency has complied with the wages and benefits, health care rate, and training rate requirements. Through a transparent process that includes public notice and public comment opportunities, the DSHS may adopt rules to establish reasonable objective criteria to temporarily exempt a home care agency determined to be facing extraordinary circumstances that prevent compliance. Consumer Directed Employer Labor Rate and the Rate-Setting Board . The consumer directed employer (CDE) labor rate must include a specific hourly amount that the CDE may only use for training, testing, and certification costs, and associated costs for the administration of training, testing, and certification scheduling and compliance for individual providers. When the board determines a proposed labor rate, it must include a specific amount for training benefits. If an agreement on a proposed CDE labor rate is not reached by a majority of the members on the board prior to August 1, then the labor rate must be determined by a majority of the voting members of the board, including a vote of the chair member. If an agreement over the labor rate is not reached prior to September 1, then the labor rate must be determined by the sole vote of the chair member. Administrative Rates and the Rate-Setting Board . The DSHS must determine a home care agency administrative rate.
