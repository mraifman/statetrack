SB 6201 · Social housing agencies/tax

Creates social housing agencies and exempts their qualifying low-income rental property from property taxes and real estate excise tax.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Real Estate Excise Tax Exemption. Adds a social housing agency to the list of qualifying grantees for the purpose of qualifying for a REET exemption. Social housing agency means a public corporation created for the purpose of developing, maintaining, and operating social housing. Social housing means mixed income housing that is made available by a social housing agency for a period of at least 99 years to primarily serve low or moderate-income households. The social housing agency must record a covenant at the time of transfer that the property will primarily be used for low-income housing for a period of at least 15 years. Property Tax Exemption. The real and personal property owned or used by a social housing agency in providing rental housing for qualifying households is exempt from taxation if at least 50 percent of the occupied dwelling units are occupied by a qualifying household and the rental housing was insured, financed, or assisted in whole or in part through one of the following: federal or state housing program administered by the Department of Commerce; federal housing program administered by a city or county; an affordable housing levy; the Washington State Housing Finance Commission; the covenant homeownership and housing related surcharges; or city or county funds designated for affordable or social housing. If less than 50 percent of the units are occupied by a qualifying household a partial exemption may be allowed for each dwelling unit occupied by a qualifying household. If at initial application the property is unoccupied, or subsequent to the initial application the property is unoccupied because of renovations, and the property is not currently being used for the exempt purposes but will be within three years the property is eligible if: a commitment for financing to acquire, construct, renovate, or otherwise convert the property to provide housing to qualifying households has been obtained; the social housing agency has manifested its intent in writing to construct, remodel, or otherwise convert the property to housing for qualifying households; and only the portion of the property that will be used to provide housing or lots for qualifying households is exempt. Qualifying household means a single person, family, or unrelated persons living together whose income is at or below 80 percent of the median income, at the time of move in, adjusted for family size for the county in which the property is located. If a currently exempt rental housing unit was occupied by a qualifying household at the time the exemption was granted and the income of the household subsequently rises above 80 percent of the median income but remains at or below 115 percent of the median income, the exemption will continue as long as the housing continues to meet the certification requirements. If a dwelling unit or lot becomes vacant or is subsequently rerented, the income of the new household must be at or below 80 percent of the median income. The social housing agency may agree to make payments to the city, county, or other political subdivision for improvements, services, and facilities furnished for the benefit of the rental housing as long as the payments do not exceed the amount last levied as the annual tax of the city, county, or political subdivision upon the property prior to exemption.
