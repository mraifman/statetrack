SB 5937 · Smart access systems/tenants

Requires landlords to offer tenants non-biometric, non-app entry options on request and limits data that smart access systems may collect.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Alternative Key Requirements . Upon request, a landlord must offer a tenant an alternative key that does not use biometric identifier information or a software application operated on the tenant's mobile phone or similar electronic device. Examples of alternative keys include, but are not limited to, key fobs, key cards, physical keys, other forms of physical access, or keypads or similar devices that use manual entering of a coded sequence of numbers, letters, or both. "Biometric identifier information" means a physiological, biological, or behavioral characteristic that is used to identify, or assist in identifying, an individual including, but not limited to: a retina or iris scan; a fingerprint; a voiceprint; or a scan or record of a palm, hand, or face geometry. Smart Access Systems . Smart access system data collection and privacy policy requirements are added to the RLTA. "Smart access system" means any system that uses electronic or computerized technology, such as a radio frequency identification card, a mobile phone application, biometric identifier information, or any other digital technology that is designed to gather information in order to grant entry to a building containing a dwelling unit, its elevators, its common areas, or to an individual dwelling unit. A smart access system does not include methods of entry that solely rely upon a keypad or similar device that uses the manual entering of a coded sequence of numbers, letters, or both. "Smart access building" means a building containing a dwelling unit that uses a smart access system. Data Collection. A smart access building landlord or third party may collect only the minimum amount of authentication data and reference data necessary to enable the use of a smart access system for the purpose of granting a user access to a building, its elevators, its common areas, or to a user's dwelling. "User" means a tenant of a smart access building. "Authentication data" means the data generated or collected at the point of authentication in connection with granting a user entry to a smart access building, common area, or dwelling unit through such building's smart access system, excluding data generated or collected by a video, audio, or camera system that is used to monitor entrances but not grant entry. "Reference data" means the information against which authentication data is verified at the point of authentication by a smart access system in order to grant a user entry to a smart access building, dwelling unit, or common area. A smart access system may collect, generate, or use only the following information: a user's name; the dwelling unit number and other doors or common areas that the user can access; a user's preferred contact method; a user's biometric identifier information if used by the smart access system; the identification card number or any identifier associated with the physical hardware used to facilitate building entry including radio frequency identification card, Bluetooth, or other similar technical protocols; passwords, passcodes, usernames, and contact information used singly or in conjunction with other reference data to grant a user entry to a smart access building, dwelling unit, or common area through the building's smart access system, or to access any online tools used to manage user accounts related to a building; lease information, including move-in and move-out dates if available; and the time and method of access, solely for security and oper
