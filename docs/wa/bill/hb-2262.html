What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

High School Civics Instruction . Beginning with or before the 2027-28 school year, the content for the mandatory, stand-alone high school civics course must include instruction in producing a legible, repeatable, official cursive signature, and how signatures are used by election officials in election, initiative, referenda, and recall processes. Students are not required to produce a legible, repeatable, official cursive signature as a condition for earning credit for the mandatory civics course. The development of applicable curricula may be coordinated with the Office of the Secretary of State and county auditors in accordance with required community outreach plans. The signature-related instruction should include an historical overview of signatures in a civics context, including how signature practices and exclusions have limited civic participation by historically marginalized communities. Election Reporting Requirements . The statewide survey of voted ballot rejection rates produced in odd-numbered years must include the number and rates of ballots that were rejected for signature mismatch by voter age groupings. The survey must also be provided to the Office of the Superintendent of Public Instruction (OSPI) for inclusion in their website content pertaining to civic education. Any election reports for the state that are published annually by the Secretary of State must also include the number and rates of ballots that were rejected for signature mismatch by voter age groupings. These reports must also be provided to the OSPI for inclusion in their website content pertaining to civic education. Substitute Bill Compared to Original Bill: In comparison to the original bill, the substitute bill: removes a requirement obligating the State Board of Education to include school district compliance with the signature-related instruction requirements in its annual monitoring of school district compliance with basic education requirements; adds that a student's signature, for purposes of the instruction requirements, may be a personalized, handwritten signature or mark that aligns with the student's primary language or culture; adds that the signature-related instruction should include an historical overview of signatures in a civics context, including how signature practices and exclusions have limited civic participation by historically marginalized communities; and adds that students are not required to produce a legible, repeatable, official cursive signature as a condition for earning credit for the mandatory high school civics course.
