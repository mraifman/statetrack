HB 2384 · Continuing care retirement

Requires continuing care retirement communities offering life care contracts to submit actuarial analyses reviewed by the Insurance Commissioner, with registration fees covering review costs.

What the bill does (WA Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

A new requirement is added to the application standards for registration as a continuing care retirement community (CCRC). Beginning July 1, 2027, if an applicant for registration as a CCRC offers life care contracts, it must provide an actuarial analysis to the Department of Social and Health Services (Department). A "life care contract" refers to a contract between a CCRC and a person to whom the CCRC agrees to provide lodging and any combination of nursing, medical, or personal care services for at least one year and up to the entirety of the person’s life. The contract is conditioned on payment of an entrance fee in lieu of, or in addition to, other recurring charges that do not change based on the care setting or services. The actuarial analysis requirement begins with the applicant's second registration renewal and applies to every other subsequent registration renewal application. The actuarial analysis must have been prepared by a qualified actuary and include an actuarial memorandum and certification. Upon receipt of an actuarial analysis in application materials, the Department must transmit it to the Office of the Insurance Commissioner (Office) for review. The Department must adopt standards for the contents of the actuarial analysis materials to be submitted. The Office must adopt standards for the review of the actuarial analyses, the qualifications of the actuaries preparing the analyses, and the administration of appeals of determinations that an applicant's actuarial balance is not satisfactory. The Office's standards must include a process for determining the applicant's ability to meet its overall contract obligations under its life care contracts in accordance with actuarial standards of practice. The standards must consider whether the applicant's actuarial balance is satisfactory and reflects liabilities that are sufficient to cover obligations under moderately adverse conditions during the testing period. The term "moderately adverse conditions" is defined as anticipating unfavorable, but not extreme, events with a reasonable probability of occurring during the testing period. Upon concluding its review, the Office must determine whether the applicant's actuarial balance is satisfactory under the review standards and notify the Department and the applicant of the results. If the Office's review finds that the applicant has not satisfied the review requirement, it will notify the applicant of its right to appeal the decision to the Office and provide the reasons for the determination in sufficient detail to allow the applicant to attempt to cure the deficiencies. For those CCRC applicants required to submit an actuarial analysis, the Department's decision to issue a registration depends on the receipt of a notice of completion of the actuarial analysis review from the Office. The Department's online listing of registered CCRCs must include a link to the results of any actuarial analysis review received by the Department from the Office. The Department may issue a provisional registration in situations in which an applicant has submitted sufficient materials for registration, but the results of the review of the actuarial analysis are pending. The Department's registration must be set at a level sufficient to cover the costs to reimburse the Office for its actuarial review activities.
