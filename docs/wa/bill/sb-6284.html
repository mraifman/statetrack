SB 6284 · AI consumer protections

Requires deployers and developers of high-risk AI systems to manage algorithmic discrimination risks, complete impact assessments, give consumer notices, and adds state disclosure duties for government AI.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

High Risk Artificial Intelligence Systems. Beginning July 1, 2027, each deployer of a high-risk AI system must use industry-standard means to protect consumers from any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination. A "high-risk artificial intelligence system" is defined as a system that is intended to autonomously make, or be a substantial factor in making, a consequential decision without meaningful human consideration. Risk Management . Developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems, on or after July 1, 2027, must implement and maintain a risk management policy and program. The policy and program must specify and incorporate the principles, processes, and personnel that the deployer uses to identify, document, and mitigate known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination. A deployer must use: a NIST or other nationally or internationally accepted framework; or comply with a framework that the attorney general designates. Impact Assessments . Deployers of high-risk AI systems, on or after July 1, 2027, must complete an impact assessment that evaluates and documents: the systems purpose; an analysis of known or foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination; steps taken to mitigate risks; a description of the data, outputs, and metrics to evaluate the system; and a description of transparency measures, post-deployment monitoring and user safeguards. Consumer Notice . Each time a deployer uses an AI system in making a consequential decision, the deployer must provide notice to the consumer with certain disclosures. In any enforcement action, there is a rebuttable presumption that a deployer used reasonable care if the deployer was in compliance with the requirements of this act. Exemptions. Developers with fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees are exempt from the risk management requirements under the act. Deployers are exempt from the risk management and impact assessment requirements if they: employ fewer than 50 full time employees; do not use the deployers own data to train the AI system; and the AI systems used by the deployer are used for the intended uses as disclosed by the deployer. The deployer must make available to consumers any impact assessment that includes information that is substantially similar to information in the impact assessment required under this act. The requirements in the act must not be construed to apply to federal agencies, the use of AI for research, performance of work in contract with certain federal agencies, HIPAA covered entities under certain circumstances, insurers regulated by the Office of the Insurance Commissioner or a comparable federal body, certain financial institutions, and other entities under specific conditions as provided in the act. Enforcement. The attorney general may bring an enforcement action under the Consumer Protection Act. Before commencing an enforcement action, the attorney general must provide 45 days written notice to a deployer or developer that is out of compliance with the act. Government Disclosure. A government agency that makes available an AI system intended to interact with consumers must provide certain disclosures to each consumer, before or at the time of interaction, that the consumer is interacting with an AI system.
