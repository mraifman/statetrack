HB 1201 · Emergency shelters/pets

Requires local emergency plans to identify pet-friendly shelters and coshelters, set safety procedures for animal sheltering, and post companion animal preparedness information online.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

When a political subdivision updates its local comprehensive emergency management plan, it is required to identify a coshelter. A "coshelter" is a temporary shelter that allows an individual to stay in an adjacent area or the same facility as a companion animal. A "companion animal" is a domesticated animal commonly kept in the home for pleasure, rather than commercial, purposes. The political subdivision must only identify the shelter to the extent practicable. A political subdivision is required to identify emergency shelters able to accommodate companion animals in its existing plan structures. An "emergency shelter" is a temporary location that provides basic shelter for individuals affected by an emergency or disaster. An "existing plan structure" is a planning document describing how a political subdivision will provide emergency shelter and temporary housing to individuals displaced by a disaster. The plan is developed in collaboration with nonprofit organizations and the state and federal government. When a political subdivision identifies an emergency shelter in response to a natural disaster or extreme weather event, it is required to identify at least one coshelter. The political subdivision must only identify the emergency shelters to the extent practicable. The emergency shelter identified by a political subdivision to accommodate companion animals is required to have safety procedures for companion animal sheltering and comply with the Federal Emergency Management Agency's (FEMA) published disaster assistance policies and procedures. A political subdivision is required to provide companion animal emergency preparedness information on its website. The information should include whether each identified emergency shelter can accommodate companion animals; information to create a companion animal evacuation plan and emergency checklist, consistent with FEMA recommendations; and identification of local organizations that provide emergency companion animal assistance.
