What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

State Environmental Policy Act Exemption . A categorical exemption from SEPA is provided for the utility-related actions of upgrading or rebuilding existing electric powerlines within an existing powerline right-of-way, including: relocations of small segments of the powerlines within an existing right-of-way or within previously disturbed or developed lands; and widening an existing powerline right-of-way to meet current electrical standards if the widening remains within previously disturbed or developed lands and only extends into a small area beyond such lands as needed to comply with applicable electrical standards. Upgrading or rebuilding existing electric powerlines includes any repair, maintenance, replacement, modification, or upgrade including, but not limited to, increases in voltage, reconductoring, installation of grid-enhancing or optimizing technologies, or the relocation or addition of utility poles, to any existing electric transmission or distribution electric powerlines and any associated infrastructure. Previously disturbed or developed refers to land that has been changed such that its functioning ecological processes have been and remain altered by human activity. The phrase encompasses areas that have been transformed from natural cover to nonnative species or a managed state including, but not limited to, utility and electric power transmission corridors and rights-of-way, and other areas where active utilities and currently used roads are readily available. This categorical exemption does not apply to proposed actions if any of the following conditions are present: The action is a series of actions related to each other, some of which are categorically exempt and some of which are not. The action is a series of exempt actions that are related to each other and that together may have a probable significant adverse environmental impact in the judgment of an agency with jurisdiction. If so, that agency must be the lead agency, unless the agencies with jurisdiction agree that another agency should be the lead agency. The agency or applicant may proceed with the exempt aspects of the proposals, prior to conducting environmental review. The action includes installation of construction directly in or under lands covered by water. For such categorically exempt projects, the utility must notify the Department of Archaeology and Historic Preservation (DAHP) and each federally recognized Indian tribe with usual and accustomed areas and ceded treaty areas where the right-of-way exists before beginning the project. A notification and consultation must allow the utility to determine that there are no existing archaeological, cultural, or tribal resources in the right-of-way. The DAHP may require a survey in coordination with the affected federally recognized Indian tribe, must ensure that the consultation occurs, and determine whether archaeological, cultural, or tribal resources are identified in an existing right-of-way. If any such resources are identified, the DAHP must ensure that the utility accounts for and protects the resources as provided under law. Information provided by the federally recognized Indian tribe must be kept confidential and exempt from public disclosure. Electric Utility Clean Energy Action Plans .
