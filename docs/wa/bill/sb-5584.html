SB 5584 · OII expansion/prosecutions

Renames the police investigations office as OIIP and creates an independent prosecutor with final charging authority over police deadly-force cases.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Orig.) (Summary of Bill))

Office of Independent Investigations. OII is renamed the Office of Independent Investigations and Prosecutions (OIIP) and is tasked with conducting fair, thorough, transparent, and competent investigations and prosecutions of police use of force and other incidents involving law enforcement as authorized by law. OIIP is authorized to conduct prosecutions of deadly force cases, including any incident involving use of deadly force by an involved officer against or upon a person who is in custody or out of custody. Appointment of Independent Prosecutor. The position of independent prosecutor (IP) is created within OIIP. The director of the OIIP must appoint an attorney to the position of IP within nine months of the effective date of the act, and to qualify for the position, a person must meet the following criteria: admission to practice law in Washington; no documented criminal or disciplinary history involving dishonesty, discrimination against person of color or marginalized communities, or abuse of power; and submission to a background check, including an assessment of criminal history and research of social media and affiliations to check for racial bias and conflicts of interest. The term of appointment for the IP is three years, and the appointed person will continue to hold the position until reappointed or until a successor is appointed. The director may remove the IP prior to the end of the term of service for misconduct or the inability to preform the duties of the office. Powers and Duties of the Independent Prosecutor. The IP is vested with final decision-making authority related to: hiring and terminating deputy independent prosecutors and related support staff; filing or declining to file criminal charges—if the IP declines to file charges after reviewing an investigation of fatal use of deadly force, the IP shall issue a written report that includes the results of the investigation and an explanation of the reasons for the decisions; and all aspects of litigation of cases within the jurisdiction of the office. The IP must ensure that individuals subjected to use of force by an involved officer, or their survivors, are kept apprised of the status of the review of the investigation, any charging decisions, all court hearings, and the status of any prosecution, including access to press conferences and advance notice of media releases. The IP may appoint assistant independent prosecutors and special assistant independent prosecutors who maintain the same powers as the IP to perform investigations and prosecutions conducted by OIIP and must establish policies and procedures to ensure that personnel with actual or apparent conflicts are screened from the review of the investigation and prosecution of criminal charges. Relationship of County Prosecuting Attorneys, the Office of the Attorney General, and the Independent Prosecutor. OIIP maintains concurrent authority and power with county prosecuting attorneys to investigate crimes and initiate and conduct prosecutions of use of deadly force by an involved officer cases and other cases under the jurisdiction of OIIP, including appeals and requests for post-conviction relief. Nothing in the act affects the authority of county prosecuting attorneys to conduct prosecutions of crimes committed by an individual who is the subject of a use of force action by the involved officer.
