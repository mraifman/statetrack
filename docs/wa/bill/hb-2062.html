HB 2062 · Maple syrup processing

Creates a biennial WSDA permitting program for maple syrup operations, with a $250 fee, and exempts permitted operators from food processor licensing and local health permits.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2025 (Summary of Bill))

Maple Syrup Processing Operations . A "maple syrup processing operation" is a person or business that produces maple syrup for sale, and includes the collection of maple sap, the processing of maple sap into maple syrup, and all related facilities, equipment, and employees. "Maple syrup" means syrup made exclusively by the evaporation of pure maple sap and does not include value added products, bottling another business' syrup, or processing other food products where maple syrup is an added ingredient. A facility that is used to collect, store, or process maple sap into maple syrup is called a "sugar shack". A sugar shack may be a permanent or temporary structure, and excludes a domestic residence. Inspection and Permitting . An inspection and permitting program is created for maple syrup processing operations. Maple syrup processing operations must be permitted by the Department of Agriculture (WSDA) every two years, with a fee of $250 for permits and permit renewals. In addition to information required by the WSDA, an applicant must provide documentation that all individuals involved in the preparation of maple syrup under a permit have appropriate food safety training, including a food and beverage service worker's permit or its equivalent. Maple syrup processing operations permitted under this program are exempt from the requirement to obtain a food processor license and from permitting and inspection by a local health jurisdiction. The WSDA may adopt rules for product labeling, hygiene and sanitary procedures, permitting, inspections, and condensation removal during the evaporation process. The WSDA may also adopt rules for facilities, water sources, and wastewater, but may not require a maple syrup processing operation to use a permanent structure as a sugar shack, install plumbing in the sugar shack, or have running water accessible on-site. Water obtained from a private source must be tested at least annually, but operations with less than $50,000 in annual gross sales that bottle syrup only and use hot water drawn from the evaporator for equipment sanitizing are exempt from water testing.
