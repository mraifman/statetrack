HB 1903 · Low-income energy assistance

Creates a state-run low-income energy assistance program administered by Commerce through participating utilities, subject to appropriation and not funded by utility surcharges.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Statewide Low-Income Energy Assistance Program . The Statewide Low-Income Energy Assistance Program (Statewide Program) is established within Commerce to reduce energy burden for low-income households in the state, and Commerce, in consultation with the UTC, must write rules to implement the program. Subject to appropriation and in consultation with an advisory group, Commerce must phase in the program across participating utilities, prioritizing low-income households in energy assistance need. Participating utilities are those who have affirmatively elected to participate in the Statewide Program and whom Commerce has accepted to participate subject to available funds. Commerce must begin providing energy assistance no later than 14 months after funding is appropriated. Energy assistance only includes monetary assistance for the purpose of the program. Commerce must, in consultation with the UTC as it applies to investor-owned utilities, administer the program by providing energy assistance funds to participating utilities and these utilities must pass the funds on to their low-income residential customers and show the energy assistance on the customers' monthly bills. Following standard contractual procedures, participating utilities may seek reimbursement from Commerce equal to the energy assistance provided. Commerce must provide a tiered amount of assistance to provide the most to the households with the greatest need. Commerce may enter into voluntary agreements with participating utilities choosing to serve as coadministrators of the Statewide Program for the purposes of enhancing customer engagement, facilitating enrollment of eligible customers, and sharing administrative duties with Commerce. The scope of a participating utility's administrative duties as a coadministrator is determined in such voluntary agreements. The Statewide Program is supplemental to low-income energy assistance provided by utilities under the requirements of CETA and gas and electric company low-income rates and programs, and participating utilities may not supplant funding from the Statewide Program to meet those requirements. A participating utility may not reduce the level of low-income energy assistance it provides as a result of participation in the Statewide Program. Enrollment . Commerce must establish enrollment details for the Statewide Program through rule making, which must consider: low-income households' eligibility; application processes for in-person, online, and over-the-phone enrollment; partnership with utilities from which low-income households receive service and the utility has chosen to serve as coadministrator; partnership with community action councils and other community providers of low-income social services; allowance for self-attestation; that Commerce may verify income qualifications; that there is no risk to eligibility based on immigration status; and auto enrollment of known eligible households. Funding . The Legislature intends for sustained funding to be provided from Climate Commitment Act auction revenues or other dollars to meet low-income household needs. Commerce's and each participating utility's obligation to provide energy assistance under the Statewide Program is based on available funding appropriated for this specific purpose.
