HB 2158 · Electronic notarial acts

Authorizes remote notarization and remote oaths for electronic notaries, sets a process for confirming tangible records, and directs DOL to adopt identity rules.

What the bill does (WA Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The 2018 RULONA is amended to specify a process for remote notarization of a tangible record not physically present before the notary and to authorize remote oaths or affirmations, as well as remote acknowledgement of a tangible record physically present before the notary. Remote Notarization of a Tangible Record Not Physically Before the Notary. To allow for remote notarization of a tangible record not physically present before the notary, the following process is specified for confirming that the record before the notary is the same record in which the remotely located individual made a statement or on which the individual executed a signature: The remotely located individual must sign the tangible record and a declaration stating that the record is the same record on which the notary performed the notarial act by means of communication technology. The notary must record the individual signing the tangible record and declaration in the required audiovisual recording. The individual must send the tangible record and declaration to the notary not later than three days after the notarial act was performed. After receiving the tangible record and declaration from the individual, the notary must execute a certificate of notarial act, which must include a statement that the notary witnessed the individual sign the tangible record and declaration by means of communication technology. A notarial act performed in compliance with this process meets the requirement to execute the certificate of notarial act contemporaneously with the performance of the notarial act and is effective on the date the remotely located individual signed the declaration. Remote Oaths or Affirmations . Unless otherwise provided by other state law, an electronic records notary public located in Washington is authorized to use communication technology to administer an oath or affirmation to a remotely located individual if the notary: identifies the remotely located individual, either through personal knowledge, by means of an oath or affirmation from a credible witness, or by using at least two different types of identity proofing; creates an audiovisual recording of the individual taking the oath or affirmation; and retains the recording in a designated repository for a period of at least 10 years. Remote Acknowledgement of a Tangible Record Physically Present Before the Notary. An electronic records notary public located in Washington may use communication technology to take an acknowledgement of a signature on a tangible record physically present before the notary if the record is displayed to and identified by the remotely located individual during the required audiovisual recording. Miscellaneous Provisions . In addition to existing rules regarding performance of a notarial act, the DOL is directed to adopt rules regarding methods for an electronic notary public to confirm the identity of a tangible record. By allowing its communication technology or identity proofing to facilitate a notarial act for a remotely located individual or by providing storage of audiovisual recordings, the provider of communication technology, identity proofing, or storage appoints the DOL Director as the provider's agent for service of process in any civil action in Washington related to the notarial act.
