HB 2145 · 340B drug pricing program

Bars drug manufacturers from restricting 340B drug delivery to covered entities and contract pharmacies, adds civil penalties, and requires annual DOH reporting.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Prohibitions . A manufacturer or third party acting on behalf of a manufacturer may not deny, restrict, or prohibit the acquisition of a 340B drug by, or delivery of a 340B drug to, a covered entity, a contract pharmacy, or any location authorized by a covered entity to receive such 340B drug, unless federal law prohibits receipt of the 340B drug. A manufacturer or third party acting on behalf of a manufacturer may not require a covered entity to submit any claims, utilization, purchasing, or other data as a condition for allowing the acquisition of a 340B drug by, or delivery of a 340B drug to, a covered entity, a contract pharmacy, or any location authorized by a covered entity to receive such 340B drug, unless federal law requires such data sharing. Enforcement . A covered entity may file a civil action against a manufacturer or a third party acting on behalf of a manufacturer for a violation of the bill's requirements relating to acquisition and delivery of 340B drugs. A court may enjoin violations and award a civil penalty of up to $5,000 per day for each violation, as well as reasonable attorneys' fees and costs. For actions brought by the Attorney General to enforce the bill's requirements relating to the acquisition and delivery of 340B drugs, a violation of the bill is deemed to affect the public interest and constitute an unfair or deceptive act in trade or commerce for purposes of the Consumer Protection Act. Reporting . A covered entity must annually report the following information, separated out by payer type, to the Department of Health (DOH) concerning the covered entity's participation in the 340B Program for the previous calendar year: certain identifying information for the covered entity; the aggregate acquisition cost and the aggregate payment amount received for all 340B drugs obtained under the 340B Program and dispensed or administered to patients; the aggregate payment made to contract pharmacies; the number of claims for prescription drugs as included in the aggregate payment amount received for all 340B drugs; how the covered entity uses any savings from participating in the 340B Program; the aggregate payments made to any other entity that is not a covered entity or a contract pharmacy for managing any aspect of the covered entity's 340B Program; the aggregate payment made for any other administering expense for the 340B Program; the aggregate number of prescription drugs dispensed or administered to patients for which a payment was reported as part of the aggregate payment amount received for all 340B drugs; the percentage of the covered entity's claims that were for prescription drugs obtained under the 340B Program; and the number and percentage of low-income patients that were served by a sliding fee scale for a prescription drug dispensed or administered under the 340B Program. A manufacturer must annually report the following information to the DOH concerning the manufacturer's participation in the 340B Program for the previous calendar year: the amount of all reductions in price available from the manufacturer, other than reductions in price required under the federal 340B statute; certain information regarding overcharges by manufacturers that exceeded the 340B ceiling price; and the average 340B discount on each of the top 25 340B drugs dispensed in the state by each manufacturer, including the percentage of the discount imposed due to inflationary rebate, and the discount if it were not capped with
