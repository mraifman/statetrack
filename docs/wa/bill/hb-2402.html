HB 2402 · Phthalates/medical equipment

Bans DEHP phthalates in IV solution containers from 2030 and IV tubing from 2035, with exemptions, shortage waivers, and a limited delay option.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Beginning January 1, 2030, a person may not manufacture, sell, or distribute into Washington, IV solution containers made with intentionally added DEHP or that contain unintentionally added DEHP in a quantity that equals or exceeds 0.1 percent weight per weight. Beginning January 1, 2035, a person may not manufacture, sell, or distribute into Washington IV tubing made with intentionally added DEHP or that contains unintentionally added DEHP in a quantity that equals or exceeds 0.1 percent weight per weight. Human blood collection and storage bags, apheresis and cell therapy blood kits and bags, including tubing, are exempt from these prohibitions. A person or entity may not replace DEHP in revised or new products with other ortho-phthalates. If the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the Department of Health (DOH) determine that there is an industry shortage of FDA-approved IV solution containers and tubing, FDA-approved IV solution containers and tubing with DEHP may be manufactured, distributed, and sold in Washington until the FDA or DOH determines the supply chain interruption is terminated. A person or entity, due to pending FDA approval for the DEHP-free IV solution containers or due to the manufacturer not having adequate equipment to manufacture the DEHP-free IV solution containers by January 1, 2030, may meet the requirement by January 1, 2032, if: the person or entity notified its Washington customers, no later than January 1, 2027, that it has commenced development of the DEHP-free IV solution containers; and the person or entity provides notice to its customers and posts to its website, no later than January 1, 2028, that it will not meet the 2030 deadline. A new chapter in Title 70A is created.
