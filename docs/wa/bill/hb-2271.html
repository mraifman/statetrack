What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

PCRC 2.0 Products . New minimum PCRC requirements are established for the following products (PCRC 2.0 products): For packaging that is a rigid plastic container, the requirement is 30 percent PCRC for products manufactured after January 1, 2029. This requirement applies to packaging with a minimum volume of 2 ounces, is capable of maintaining its shape when empty, is comprised primarily of one or more plastic resins, and is not used in certain direct food contact applications. For the following rigid plastic products, the requirement is 30 percent PCRC for products manufactured after January 1, 2029: (a) buckets and pails; (b) household storage and organization containers and lids; (c) waste containers and lids other than roll carts; (d) nursery pots or trays; (e) bulk shipping or storage containers; (f) household cleaning containers; and (g) indoor and outdoor furniture. For roll carts, the requirement is 10 percent PCRC for those manufactured beginning January 1, 2029, and 30 percent PCRC for those manufactured beginning January 1, 2031. For film plastics manufactured beginning January 1, 2029, the requirement is 10 percent PCRC. Fifteen categories of container or product types are exempted from PCRC 2.0 product requirements, including packaging in direct contact with certain foods, beverages, and medicines; packaging for infant formula, medical food, and oral nutritional supplements; packaging for hazardous or flammable products; and compostable packaging. Requirements for PCRC 1.0 Products . A variety of changes are made to PCRC requirements for PCRC 1.0 products, including that: PCRC requirements are specified to apply based on the date when a PCRC 1.0 product is manufactured. Plastic beverage container PCRC requirements are specified to apply only to hermetically sealed or otherwise capped and airtight containers other than cups. The minimum size of personal household cleaning and personal care product containers subject to PCRC requirements is reduced from 8 ounces to 2 ounces. Ecology may adjust the minimum thickness of plastic trash bags to a thickness of less than 0.7 mils if a significant portion of the market redesigns to thinner bags. Producers of plastic trash bags must begin labeling the packaging containing the bags with the minimum PCRC content, beginning in 2027. Plastic trash bag producers must submit a third-party certificate of compliance with PCRC requirements to Ecology. A decision by Ecology to adjust the minimum PCRC requirement for a PCRC 1.0 product must take effect 24 months after an Ecology determination to adjust the rate, rather than applicable in the year immediately following the decision. Any exclusions from PCRC requirements for household cleaning and personal care products regulated under the federal Insecticide, Rodenticide and Fungicide Act (FIFRA) must be granted for a minimum period of four years. Provisions Applicable to Both PCRC 1.0 Products and PCRC 2.0 Products . The producer of a PCRC 1.0 product or PCRC 2.0 product is specified to be: the manufacturer, brand or trademark owner or licensee, brand owner, importer into the United States, or distributor into the state, in descending order of responsibility. De minimis producers that have an inflation-adjusted annual global gross revenue of $5 million are not subject to PCRC requirements for PCRC 1.0 products or PCRC 2.0 products, but must register and report to Ecology in the same manner as other producers.
