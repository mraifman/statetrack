SB 5633 · Subdivision of land

Streamlines plat and subdivision approval by limiting required public hearings, meetings and design review, and allowing administrative approval with notice to neighbors.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The bill as referred to committee not considered. Summary of Bill (First Substitute): Subdivision statutes do not apply to a division made for the purpose of alteration by adjusting boundary lines which does not create a lot, tract, or divisions that is not buildable according to locally adopted development regulations for critical areas, shorelines, stormwater, setbacks, impervious surface areas, or building coverage standards. A city, town, or county may not require a public predecision meeting, or any design review other than administrative design review, for plats and subdivisions except for those required to comply with state law. A preapplication conference may only be required when consistent with the project permit application provisions. If a preapplication conference for a complete application is required, the preapplication conference must: include discussion of the city, town, or county requirements for obtaining a determination of completeness and the applicable review processes, permit types, and development regulations; and answer questions from the applicant to facilitate preparation and submission of a complete application. Applications for approval of rezones, variances, planned unit developments, site plan approvals, development agreements, and any other similar quasi-judicial or administrative actions must be processed concurrently with an application for preliminary plat approval to the extent that procedural requirements allow for concurrent processing. Upon filing a complete application for preliminary plat approval, the local government must provide notice to adjacent landowners of property located within 250 feet as provided in development regulations adopted by the city, town, or county. A public hearing for approval of a plat application for a proposed subdivision may not be required, but a city, town, or county may provide for a public hearing. A county, city or town may adopt an ordinance allowing for approval of a preliminary plat application administratively without a public hearing, including setting a threshold number of lots in a subdivision requiring a public hearing and other factors that necessitate a public hearing. Notice requirements of the administrative process must be set forth in development regulations, and may include, but are not limited to, the following: requiring notice be posted on or around the land proposed to be subdivided to attract public awareness of the proposal; mailing notice of the proposed subdivision to neighboring property owners located within at least 200 feet of the site, including notification that no public hearing will be held on the application, except under certain circumstances; and the process and time requirements for the public to comment on the proposed subdivision and for the applicant to respond to the comments in writing. A city, town, or county may specify in its development regulations when a public hearing must be held and, if such a hearing is required, the development regulations must set forth the requirements for notice of the public hearing, by whom the hearing will be conducted, and the procedures for the hearing. The administrative personnel must review the public use and interest proposed to be served by the establishment of the subdivision and dedication.
