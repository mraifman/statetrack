Renames and restructures the poverty reduction task force and advisory council, adds state agency members, and redefines their duties around the 10-year poverty plan.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The Legislative-Executive WorkFirst Poverty Reduction Oversight Task Force is renamed the Legislative-Executive Economic Justice and Well-Being Task Force (Task Force). The number of voting members of the Task Force are expanded from 8 to 11 to add the following state agencies: the Department of Revenue; the Health Care Authority; and the Workforce Training and Education Coordinating Board. The Task Force cochairs must be selected from the voting members and serve two-year terms. The Task Force representatives from the Governor's Offices of Equity, Indian Affairs, and Financial Management are identified as nonvoting members. The duties of the Task Force are modified to specify that the Task Force: oversees the budget and performance of the WorkFirst program; determines outcome and accountability measures for the 10-year plan to dismantle poverty; collaborates with the advisory council to monitor the strategies and recommendations in the 10-year plan to dismantle poverty; seeks input for poverty reduction to encourage the development of cross-sector and innovative approaches to poverty reduction; analyzes data with a primary focus on the strategies and recommendations of the 10-year plan to dismantle poverty; and collaborates with and seeks advice from the Washington Economic Justice Alliance in supporting the 10-year plan to dismantle poverty. Economic Justice and Well-Being Advisory Council . The Intergenerational Poverty Advisory Committee is renamed the Economic Justice and Well-Being Advisory Council (Council). The Secretary of the Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS) is authorized to appoint members to the Council as appropriate (in addition to the current members). The required membership of the Council is modified to: include organizations that address poverty, education, or early childhood education grants that may include faith-based organizations, but is not limited to faith-based organizations; include individuals impacted by poverty; remove a subject matter expert in infant mental health; remove a representative from the Department of Children, Youth, and Families and the DSHS; and add entities representing the workforce system and postsecondary education systems. The duties of the Council are modified to require that it monitor the strategies and recommendations outlined in the 10-year plan to dismantle poverty and recommend to the Task Force priorities and policy options that the Task Force should consider proposing to the Governor and the Legislature to effectively reduce poverty and promote and encourage self-sufficiency.
