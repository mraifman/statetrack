HB 1154 · Solid waste handling

Requires Ecology to review and approve landfill permits and sets procedures for Ecology to take over enforcement from local health districts.

What the bill does (WA Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Solid Waste Permits . Local governments have the primary responsibility for issuing the permits required by this chapter, administering the regulatory program consistent with the policy and provisions of this chapter, and imposing penalties for violations. Ecology must review and approve every permit or renewal of a permit for landfilling prior to issuance or renewal by the JHD. Ecology may require a JHD to amend a proposed landfill permit to ensure conformance with applicable laws and regulations. After August 1, 2027, any permits for landfilling must be approved by Ecology to be valid. Both the JHD and Ecology may at any time suspend a permit for a solid waste disposal site if either determines that the site or the facility are being operated in violation of any applicable solid waste management laws or regulations. An applicant or permittee must allow the JHD and Ecology to conduct inspections and collect samples. Solid Waste Enforcement . Ecology must take enforcement action if a JHD sends written notice to Ecology that it is deferring to Ecology's authority to take enforcement action or Ecology determines that a JHD's enforcement action is inadequate to address violations by a solid waste handling facility operator. If the JHD initiates an enforcement action within 30 calendar days of the JHD's receipt of Ecology's notice of intent to enforce that Ecology and the JHD agree will adequately address the violations, Ecology must hold its enforcement action in abeyance if the violation does not present an immediate and substantial endangerment to human health or the environment. If Ecology determines that a JHD enforcement action is inadequate and that it will take enforcement action, Ecology must provide written notice of its intent to enforce to the JHD and to the solid waste handling facility operator. Ecology's notice of intent to enforce must be provided at least 30 calendar days prior to issuing a penalty or order unless the violation presents an immediate and substantial endangerment to human health or the environment. Ecology's notice of intent to enforce must include: identification of the alleged violations of the statute, regulation, or rule that are the basis for Ecology's enforcement action and the number of alleged violations; a description of Ecology's process that led to its determination that such violations existed; which criteria apply; the proposed start date and any end date of Ecology's enforcement action; and the proposed geographical boundaries of solid waste handling facilities at which the enforcement action is planned.
