What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

Credentialed health care providers who provide health care services must wear a visible identification badge during all patient encounters. The badge must include the health care provider's name, health care credential, and the initials of any health care-related degrees that they hold. The identification badge requirement does not apply to health care providers providing custodial services or who work in nonpatient settings and do not have any direct patient contact. Any advertisement for health care services that names a health care provider must also include the type of health care credential that the health care provider holds as well as the initials and title of any health care-related degrees. A violation of the identification badge or advertisement requirements is considered unprofessional conduct. Hospitals, assisted living facilities, nursing homes, adult family homes, and ambulatory surgical facilities must provide updated badges to health care providers upon the occurrence of several specified events. An updated identification badge is required: upon hiring a new health care provider to provide health care services; upon a new identification badge photograph being required; upon the name change of a health care provider; upon a health care provider's receipt of a new health care credential or health care-related degree; when a new credentialing period begins; or on January 1, 2030. Persons are prohibited from representing themselves as either an "osteopathic physician surgeon" or a "physician surgeon" unless they: are either a licensed physician or osteopathic physician; have completed a surgical residency or fellowship approved by the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education or the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada; are certified or eligible for examination by a surgical board of the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education or the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada; and maintain continuing education and proficiency in their specialty. A violation of the title protection provisions for physicians and osteopathic physicians is considered unlicensed practice.
