What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Risks, Strengths, and Needs Assessment Tool . The Department of Children, Youth, and Families (DCYF) is required to conduct a study to improve its risks, strengths, and needs assessment tool used in the risk assessment process when investigating alleged child abuse and neglect referrals to: identify family risks, strengths, cultural strengths, and needs; identify the risk of harm to a child when a substance use disorder is a factor in the case or high potency synthetic opioids are a condition in the home that incorporates guidance from the Department of Health regarding high potency synthetic opioids and child safety; determine the culturally appropriate service needs of the family and support caseworkers to identify and transition families to the appropriate services quickly; identify the risk of harm to a child when domestic violence is a factor in the home; support reassessment and monitoring of family progress; and ensure that the assessment tool incorporates evidence-based practices to address cultural bias. By November 1, 2026, the DCYF must provide data and information to the Legislature on its efforts to improve the risks, strengths, and needs assessment tool, including feedback provided by the Office of Tribal Relations within the DCYF. Risk Assessment Process when Investigating Child Abuse and Neglect . The DCYF risk assessment process used when investigating child abuse and neglect referrals must be certified at least once every three years based on current academic standards for assessment validation. This certification may be completed by the Office of Innovation, Alignment, and Accountability or an outside researcher. Second Substitute Bill Compared to Original Bill: The second substitute bill modifies the requirements for the study to improve the Department of Children, Youth, and Families (DCYF) risks, strengths, and needs assessment tool to: include cultural strengths; incorporate guidance from the Department of Health regarding high potency synthetic opioids and child safety; determine the culturally appropriate service needs of the family; identify the risk of harm to a child when domestic violence is a factor in the home; incorporate evidence-based practices to address cultural bias; and include feedback from the Office of Tribal Relations within the DCYF. The second substitute bill modifies the date of the DCYF reporting of data and information regarding the risks, strengths, and needs assessment tool by one year to November 1, 2026 (instead of November 1, 2025). The second substitute bill makes technical changes.
