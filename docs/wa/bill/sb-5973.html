What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Signature Gathering. It is unlawful to provide or receive money or other consideration for signature gathering based on the number of signatures obtained on an initiative or referendum petition. A person who has reason to believe that a person is providing or receiving money or other consideration for signature gathering based on the number of signatures may bring a citizen's action in the name of the state for an appropriate civil remedy, including civil penalties, against any initiative or referendum measure sponsor or signature-gather entity that violates the prohibition. If the person who brings the citizen's action prevails, the judgment awarded must go to the state, but the person must be entitled to recover reasonable attorneys' fees and costs from the defendant as fixed by the court. In the case of a citizen's action that is dismissed and that the court also finds was brought frivolously or in bad faith, the court may order the person bringing the action to pay all reasonable attorneys' fees incurred by the defendant. Civil penalties may not exceed $10,000 for each violation. The action for violation of the prohibition may be brought in the superior court of the county of residence or principal place of business of the initiative or referendum measure sponsor or signature-gathering entity or the superior court of the county in which a violation occurred. Any action must be commenced within two years after the date when the violation occurred. A signature-gathering entity is a person or entity that is employed, paid, or otherwise receives consideration or gratuity for the purpose of soliciting or procuring signatures of voters on an initiative or referendum petition. Minimum Signature Requirement. If any legal voter of the state, either individually or on behalf of an organization, desires to petition the Legislature to enact a proposed initiative measure, or submit a proposed initiative measure to the people, they must file with SOS a petition, bearing the signatures of at least 1000 legal voters in support of the proposed measure, including the legal voter's name and the address, city, and county at which they are registered to vote. SOS must, prior to submitting a copy of the proposed measure to the CRO, verify and canvass the signatures and names of the legal voters. SOS must by rule establish procedures to implement this signature requirement. SOS may use statistical sampling techniques to verify whether a petition contains the required number of signatures of legal voters. SOS may not accept for filing any proposed measure if the legal voter failed to submit a petition bearing the required signatures or if the petition clearly bears insufficient signatures. The signature sheets used to collect signatures must meet all formatting and content requirements, except for the inclusion of the title.
