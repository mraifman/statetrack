What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Patronizing a Person for Prostitution . The offense of Patronizing a Prostitute is renamed to Patronizing a Person for Prostitution. The means of committing the offense are expanded. A person is guilty of Patronizing a Person for Prostitution if, instead of paying a fee, the person: provides anything of value, pursuant to a prior understanding, as compensation for another person having engaged in sexual conduct with him or her; provides anything of value to another person with the understanding that the person will engage in sexual conduct with him or her in return; or solicits or requests another person to engage in sexual conduct with him or her in exchange for providing anything of value. Patronizing a Person for Prostitution is a gross misdemeanor for a person's first two convictions and a class C felony for a person's third or subsequent conviction, provided that the prior convictions were for conduct that occurred after April 1, 2026. Additional fees assessed for Patronizing a Person for Prostitution are increased to: $3,000 for a first offense; $5,000 for a second offense; and $10,000 for a third or subsequent offense. At least 98 percent of the revenue from the fees assessed for Patronizing a Person for Prostitution must be directed to community-based, survivor-led organizations that provide rehabilitative services, such as mental health and substance abuse counseling, parenting skills, training, housing relief, education, vocational training, drop-in centers, and employment counseling. Two percent of the revenue from the fees assessed must be remitted quarterly to the Department of Commerce with a report detailing the fees assessed, the revenue received, and how that revenue was spent. Prostitution . The means of committing the offense of Prostitution are expanded to include if a person engages, agrees to engage, or offers to engage in sexual conduct with another person in return for accepting anything of value. Law enforcement must refer a person to services for a person's first and second investigative detention for Prostitution. A third or subsequent violation is a misdemeanor. Assault of a person engaged in prostitution is a class C felony. Law enforcement may not arrest a person for Prostitution when the person is reporting the commission of another crime or when law enforcement is investigating another crime.
