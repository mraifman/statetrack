Exempts EFSEC's government-to-government tribal consultations from open meetings rules and requires the chair and staff to offer consultation, share summaries, and let tribes correct them.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

During the site certification process, Government-To-Government consultation between EFSEC and federally-recognized tribes is exempted from the OPMA if there is no deliberation between, and no assurances or commitments are made by EFSEC members. Tribal consultation during the site certification process is amended to require: the EFSEC chair and designated staff offer to conduct tribal consultation with relevant tribes to seek ways to address issues of concern raised by such a tribe and, upon request of the tribe to meet with all EFSEC members, to include as many members as possible; and EFSEC provide its summary of consultation to the relevant tribe before it is submitted in the recommendation report, allowing the tribe 30 days to request corrections or additional information or to provide its own summary for inclusion in the recommendations report. EFFECT OF CHANGES MADE BY ENVIRONMENT, ENERGY & TECHNOLOGY COMMITTEE (First Substitute): Provides that, upon receipt of an application for site certification, the chair and designated staff—rather than all EFSEC members—must offer to conduct tribal consultation, but if the tribe requests to meet with all EFSEC members, tribal consultation must include as many members as possible.
