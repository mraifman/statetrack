HB 2640 · Unauthorized UCC filings

Allows the Department to refuse or terminate suspected fraudulent UCC financing statements, creates review and reinstatement processes, and establishes criminal penalties for fraudulent filings.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The Department may refuse to accept an initial or amended financing statement for filing if it was not filed by a trusted filer and the Department reasonably believes that the record was communicated to the Department with the intent to harass or defraud the person identified as the debtor. If the record is subsequently accepted based on additional information, the record is effective as if it was not initially refused, except as against a purchaser of the collateral that reasonably relied upon the absence of the record. A "trusted filer" is a person that regularly causes records to be communicated to the filing office through batch filing and has provided the office with current contact information and information sufficient to establish the person's identity. A process is created for termination of a wrongfully filed financing statement. The Department may file a termination statement with respect to a financing statement if it receives an affidavit from a person identified as the debtor in the financing statement indicating that the filed record was not authorized and was communicated to the Department with the intent to harass or defraud the debtor. The affidavit must contain specified information and be signed under penalty of perjury. The Department may reject an affidavit that it believes was delivered with the intent to harass or defraud the secured party. If the financing statement that is the subject of the affidavit was submitted by a trusted filer, the Department must first conduct an expedited administrative review to determine whether it was submitted with intent to harass or defraud the debtor. A filed termination statement is not effective until 30 days after it is filed. The Department must notify the secured party under the financing statement that the termination statement has been filed and will become effective 30 days after filing. Notice must be sent by certified mail on the day the termination statement is filed, with a copy sent to the electronic mail address provided by the secured party. Before a termination statement takes effect, a secured party who believes the filed record was authorized may request that the Department conduct an expedited review of the filed record and any provided documentation. Based on the expedited review, the Department may remove the filed termination statement from the record and conduct an administrative review. If the Department determines after an administrative review that the financing statement was submitted with intent to harass or defraud the debtor, the Department must promptly file a termination statement, which takes effect immediately, and send notice of the termination to the secured party. The secured party may file an action against the Department seeking reinstatement of a terminated financing statement. The action must be filed no later than six months after the date on which the termination statement takes effect. If the court determines the financing statement was authorized and not communicated with the intent to harass or defraud the debtor, the court must order reinstatement of the financing statement. If the court finds that the filed record was communicated with the intent to harass or defraud the debtor, the Department and the debtor may recover from the secured party their costs and reasonable attorneys' fees incurred in the action.
