HB 2430 · Crime victim penalty assess.

Raises crime victim penalty assessments to $1,000 for felonies and $500 for misdemeanors, allows surcharges up to $50,000 for wealthy defendants, and limits waivers to indigent defendants.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The CVPA is $1,000 in the case of a felony or gross misdemeanor offense and $500 in the case of a misdemeanor offense. The court must impose the CVPA unless the defendant provides documentary proof, and the court finds on the record at the sentencing hearing, that the defendant is indigent. The court may impose an additional surcharge on the penalty assessment if the court finds that the defendant possesses substantial financial resources, which is defined as having a household income that exceeds 200 percent of the area median income. Upon motion by the prosecutor that the defendant may possess substantial financial resources, the court must order the defendant to provide proof of all forms of income prior to the sentencing hearing. The court may impose an additional surcharge on the penalty assessment up to $50,000 if the court finds by a preponderance of the evidence that the defendant possesses substantial financial resources. The court is advised to impose a surcharge that is proportionate to the severity of the offense and the harm to the victim and the community. The surcharge received must be transferred to the county treasurer and deposited into the same fund as the CVPA amounts received. The court must require payment of the CVPA and any surcharge imposed as a condition to the suspension of a sentence or of probation. In a case of noncompliance or contempt due to a person's failure to pay LFOs, the CVPA and surcharge may not be reduced, waived, or converted to community restitution hours unless the person is indigent. Substitute Bill Compared to Original Bill: The substitute bill, compared to the original bill: increases the CVPA from $500 to $1,000 in the case of a felony or gross misdemeanor offense and from $250 to $500 in the case of a misdemeanor offense; authorizes the court to impose an additional surcharge on the CVPA up to $50,000 if the court finds that the defendant possesses substantial financial resources; requires the defendant to provide documentary proof of indigency and for the court to find at the sentencing hearing and on the record that the defendant is indigent before exempting the defendant from the CVPA; prohibits the CVPA and any additional surcharge from being reduced, waived, or converted to community restitution hours unless the defendant is indigent; requires payment of the CVPA and any additional surcharge imposed as a condition to the suspension of a sentence or of probation; and restores the exception to the CVPA for an indigent person convicted of an offense for which the CVPA must be imposed, striking language in the original bill that removes the exception for a person convicted of a domestic violence offense, sex offense against a child, or a crime against persons.
