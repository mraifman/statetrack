HB 2440 · Education ombuds records

Makes identifying information from education ombuds complainants confidential, limits its disclosure, and gives complainants access to their own records.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Identifying information of an individual from whom the Office of the Education Ombuds (OEO) obtains information in connection with a complaint: (a) is confidential and not subject to subpoena, discovery, or other compulsory process; and (b) may not be disclosed to any person or entity except as authorized under specified circumstances. The OEO may disclose identifying information in connection with a complaint only under one or more of the following circumstances: (a) the individual whose identifying information is at issue provides written consent to the disclosure; or (b) the disclosure is in compliance with a legislative subpoena or a request from the Governor and is necessary for an investigation or inquiry into alleged neglect of duty or misconduct by the Education Ombuds or the OEO. Upon written request, the OEO must provide a complainant with access to their own complaint and other records maintained by the OEO that both contain the complainant's identifying information and that relate to the complaint. Before disclosing this information to the complainant, the Education Ombuds must redact identifying information of any other individual and information that is confidential or privileged under state or federal law. A written request by a complainant to disclose their own records does not make these records subject to disclosure under the Public Records Act. The Education Ombuds is not prohibited from disclosing deidentified data, without written consent, to the Education Research and Data Center, consistent with applicable security and confidentiality requirements.
