What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Orig.) (Summary of Bill))

Flexible Demand Programs. Electric utilities with more than 100,000 electric customers must implement a flexible demand program by December 31, 2026. All other electric utilities may choose to implement a flexible demand program. A flexible demand program is defined as an electric utility program that encourages consumers to shift or modulate their electricity demand in ways that benefit the electric utility's electric system. This may include incentives, time of use rates, virtual power plants, and other mechanisms for signaling end use load and customer energy storage systems to respond to price signals. To be approved for a residential battery incentive program (incentive program), a flexible demand program must: provide an upfront battery incentive payment; a plan for incorporating residential battery energy storage systems and connected distributed energy resources into a flexible demand program and financially encourage customers to manage their electricity by responding to time of use rates or demand charge price signals to reduce costs to the electrical system, or provide the utility with the means to manage batteries collectively to benefit the grid; allow a user of a net metering system to fully participate in the program; not allow for battery energy storage system leases to customers unless they are utility-owned rent-to-own, or owned by a electric utility and leased at no cost to low-income or moderate-income customers; be designed to lower a customer's current total annual electric utility expenses; and reserve at least 40 percent of the program funding for low-income, moderate-income, or tribal households. Residential Battery Incentive Grant Program. Subject to the availability of amounts appropriated, a residential battery incentive grant program (grant program) is established to improve access to residential battery energy storage systems for low-income and moderate-income customers, and maximize system benefits for all retail electric customers. The Department of Commerce (Commerce) must award grants to electric utilities with qualified incentive programs. In a biennium, no more than 35 percent may go to a single utility and no more than 40 percent may be awarded to investor-owned utilities. If Commerce awards funds in multiple rounds, the funding caps do not apply in a final funding round. Commerce's application process for electric utilities establishing an incentive program must include: plans for how the utility's incentive program will encourage low-income participation; an example of customer application that requires one of several specified income verification processes at the time of enrollment; a statement on the types of utility rent-to-own or leased agreements for residential battery energy systems; expected monthly savings to participating customers' electrical bill expenses; descriptions for prioritizing customer applications to the flexible demand program and using customer feedback to design the incentive program; documentation of utility expenses and upgrades proposed as part of program implementation, which may not exceed 20 percent of total grant funds received by a utility during a biennium, and may include advanced metering infrastructure and subscription fees paid by the utility to operators of an electric utility-owned virtual power plant; and a notarized signed and sworn statement that customer data will not be sold or aggregated for any purposes beyond the direct operation of its flexible deman
