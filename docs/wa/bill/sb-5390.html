SB 5390 · Access to recreation sites

Raises the Discover Pass fee to $45, adds a third plate space, and makes the lifetime disabled veteran pass equivalent to a Discover Pass.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Discover Pass. The cost of a Discover Pass is increased from $30 to $45. The threshold for revenue distribution changes for the biennium is increased from $71 million to $100 million. Beginning July 1, 2026, the Discover Pass must contain space for three motor vehicle license plate numbers, instead of two. State Parks—Park Passes—Lifetime Veteran's Disability Pass. A Lifetime Disabled Veteran Pass is equivalent to, and entitles the holder to all the benefits of, a Discover Pass, including access to recreation site or lands. The exemption under current law, for recipients of a Lifetime Veteran's Disability Pass from payment of campsite reservation fees, is removed. Pass or Permit—Requirements. A Lifetime Disabled Veteran Pass is added to the list of passes which may be displayed on the front windshield or in a prominent location of a vehicle operating on or parking at any recreation site or lands. A Pass or Permit is not required for a motor vehicle used for off-road recreation that has been transported to a recreation site or lands by another motor vehicle that remains parked and displays a Pass or Permit. Proof of obtaining a Lifetime Disabled Veteran Pass may be provided as an alternative to obtaining a Discover Pass, for an individual to be eligible for the reduced penalty rate of $59. The Parks and Recreation Commission may deny or revoke any Washington State park pass, at any time, for cause.
