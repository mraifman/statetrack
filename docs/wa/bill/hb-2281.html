HB 2281 · State-tribal relationship

Requires state agencies to consult tribes before actions affecting tribal cultural places and bars undue burdens on traditional practices absent a compelling interest.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Government-to-Government Consultation . General. The government-to-government consultation process between a state agency and a tribe must be mutually agreed upon. "Indian tribe" is redefined as any federally recognized Indian tribe with traditional lands and territories in Washington. Tribal Traditional Cultural Places. Before taking any agency action that may affect a tribal traditional cultural place, a state agency must comply with the consultation requirements of Executive Order 21-02. "Agency actions" are defined as projects proposed in state-funded construction or acquisition projects covered by Executive Order 21-02. "Tribal traditional cultural place" is defined as any geophysical or geographical place, area, or feature associated with the cultural practices, traditions, beliefs, lifeways, arts, crafts, or social institutions of an Indian tribe. "Traditional" is defined as those beliefs, customs, and practices of an Indian tribe that have been passed down through generations and the places associated with those beliefs, customs, and practices. Cause of Action . A state agency may not impose an undue burden or interfere with an Indian tribe's exercise of traditional cultural practices on a tribal traditional cultural place unless it is in furtherance of a compelling governmental interest and is the least restrictive means of furthering that compelling governmental interest. An Indian tribe may bring a claim in superior court for a violation of this requirement. An action against a state agency must be commenced within three years of the agency action. Burden of Proof. If the tribal traditional cultural place or practice can be disclosed, the tribe has the burden of proving that the agency action poses an undue burden on a tribal traditional cultural practice or on the management, use, or preservation of a tribal traditional cultural place. If the tribe meets this burden, the agency must then prove that the governmental interest in the state action is compelling and that the agency chose the least intrusive means for satisfying that governmental interest. If a tribe's traditional cultural tenets prohibit disclosure about a tribal traditional cultural place or practice, it is the agency's burden to prove that: it has a compelling governmental interest in pursuing the action; it is essential that the agency's compelling governmental issue be furthered as originally proposed; and none of the less intrusive alternatives identified adequately advance that compelling governmental interest. An agency may not proceed with the proposed action if it does not meet its burden of proof. Substitute Bill Compared to Original Bill: The substitute bill redefines "agency action" to mean projects proposed in state-funded construction or acquisition projects covered by Executive Order 21-02. The substitute bill removes the reference to "contemporary" lands in the definition of "Indian tribe." The substitute bill eliminates the consultation requirements for an agency action that may affect a tribal traditional cultural place in the original bill and instead requires state agencies to comply with the consultation requirements of Executive Order 21-02 before taking any agency action that may affect a tribal traditional cultural place.
