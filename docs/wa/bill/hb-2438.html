Creates the SEEDS Scholarship, funded at $10 million a year from the GET account, for low-to-moderate income students in early childhood education programs who commit to working in early learning centers.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Beginning in the 2027-28 academic year, the Supporting Our Early Education Degree Seekers (SEEDS) Scholarship is established to provide up to 90 credits of tuition-free community or technical college to eligible students enrolled in eligible early childhood education stackable certificates or associate degree programs. Students must commit to working in an early learning center for at least two years upon completion of their program. Awards are the difference between a recipient's tuition and services and activities fees, less the value of any state-funded grant, scholarship, gift aid, and waiver assistance for up to 90 credits to be earned within three academic years for students without an associate's degree, and up to 30 credits for students who have already earned an associate's degree. Awards also include student support and wraparound services, provided by each community or technical college, to reduce or help to reduce barriers that threaten completion of eligible degree or certificate programs. The Office of Student Financial Assistance (Office) is tasked with implementing and administering the SEEDS Scholarship, and in doing so may collaborate with a statewide childcare resource and referral organization. Starting August 1, 2027, the amount of $10 million must be annually allocated from the GET Account, but only if the actuarial funded status remains at or above 120 percent. Funding must be used solely for providing SEEDS Scholarship awards, including support and wraparound services, and up to $500,000 annually for administration costs. The Caseload Forecast Council must estimate the anticipated caseload of the SEEDS Scholarship. To be eligible, a resident student must meet the following requirements: have a family income at or between 65 percent to 150 percent of the state median family income; be enrolled at least part time in a community or technical college in an eligible degree or certificate program; complete the Free Application for Federal Student Aid or the Washington Application for State Financial Aid for the academic year in which the student seeks eligibility for the scholarship; sign an attestation to work in an early learning center for at least two years upon completion of an eligible degree or certificate program; and maintain satisfactory academic progress, as determined by the Office. Any claims challenging the validity of the act not filed before January 1, 2030, are barred. The WSAC is required to send notifications to each owner of a tuition unit contract about the purchasing of tuition units by organizations for scholarships. The notification must also include information about the SEEDS Scholarship and specifically the statute of limitations. The WSAC must collaborate with the SBCTC to conduct a study on the sustainability of the SEEDS Scholarship that includes any increase in enrollments at community or technical colleges, any decreases in enrollments elsewhere at institutions of higher education, and changes in recipient persistence, completion, and time-to-degree rates in eligible degree or certificate programs. The study must also include an analysis on the demand for student services, such as advising and student success courses, recommendations to improve student services and outcomes, and the financial impact of the SEEDS Scholarship on recipients.
