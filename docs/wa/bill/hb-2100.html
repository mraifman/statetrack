What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

High Earners Payroll Expense Tax on Large Operating Companies . Beginning July 1, 2026, a payroll expense tax is levied on large operating companies engaged in business within Washington. The tax is 5 percent of the total annual employee wages that exceed the Social Security wage limit. The tax is imposed on the employer. A “large operating company” is a company that employs more than 20 people, maintains more than $5 million in gross receipts or sales, and maintains an address in the United States. The payroll expense tax does not apply to any employer with total employee wages equal to or less than $7 million for the prior calendar year. An employer may not make any deductions from employee wages to pay for the tax. The tax is administered by the Employment Security Department (ESD). The ESD must annually set the employee wage amount excluded from payroll expense tax equal to the maximum wages subject to tax for Social Security. A credit is allowed against the state payroll expense tax for any eligible city payroll expense tax paid by the employer. The amount of credit is equal to the total amount of eligible city payroll expense tax remitted to the city during the prior state tax reporting period. The credit may not exceed the tax that would be owed for the city payroll expense tax in effect on January 1, 2024. "Eligible city payroll expense tax" means a tax that is measured by annual employee compensation and imposed by a city, as of January 1, 2024. Beginning January 1, 2027, the ESD must collect penalties and interest on delinquent taxes in the same manner as required for the PFML program. Other administrative and regulatory provisions include: employer reporting and records retention requirements; successor liability provisions; the process to handle delinquencies and collection of unpaid taxes; filing of liens; authorization for the ESD to settle claims for tax interest and penalties; and administrative appeals and judicial review. Well Washington Fund . The Well Washington Fund Account (WWFA) is created in the state treasury. Beginning July 1, 2027, 51 percent of revenues from the payroll expense tax will be deposited into the WWFA. Moneys may only be spent after appropriation and may only be used for higher education programs, health care, cash assistance programs, and energy and housing programs. The remainder of the tax revenues will be deposited into the State General Fund. The Well Washington Fund Oversight and Accountability Board (Board) is established and will consist of 25 members. The Board members shall include: ten total members from the House of Representatives (House) who will be selected from each of the two major caucuses from the following five committees (or successor committees): Appropriations, Early Learning and Human Services, Health Care and Wellness, Environment and Energy, and Housing; ten total members from the Senate, who will be selected from each of the two major caucuses from the following five committees (or successor committees): Ways and Means, Human Services, Health and Long-term Care, Higher Education and Workforce Development, and Environment, Energy, and Technology; and. five members, appointed by the Governor and confirmed by the Senate, that have expertise on the intersection of federal and state funding in the following five areas of public policy: higher education, health care, cash assistance programs, housing, and the environment.
