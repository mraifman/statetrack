What the bill does (WA Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The Uniform Consumer Debt Default Judgments Act (Act) is adopted. The Act establishes requirements that a plaintiff must follow to obtain a default judgment in an action to collect a consumer debt. "Consumer debt" means an obligation to pay money that arises out of a transaction in which the money, property, insurance, or service that is the subject of the transaction is primarily for a personal, family, or household purpose. Requirements for a Complaint . A default judgment in an action for collection of a consumer debt may be entered only if the complaint or amended complaint includes a variety of specific information relating to the consumer, the creditor, and the debt being collected, including: the name of the creditor, including any merchant or affinity brand or facility name, and for medical debt, the name of the provider or facility associated with the debt; at least the last four digits of the account number or identifier used by the creditor in communicating to the consumer; the date of last payment or last transaction, and the date of charge off or default; the amount of the outstanding balance and judgment sought, including an itemization of amounts for finance charges, fees, and credits and payments; whether the judgment amount may increase due to accrued interest, attorneys' fees, or other amounts; the authority of the plaintiff to commence the action; and facts demonstrating that the action is commenced in the proper venue and within the applicable statute of limitations. If the action is to recover purchased debt, the complaint must state additional information, including: the date the debt was purchased; the identity of the person from which the debt was purchased; that the plaintiff may have purchased the debt for less than the value named in the complaint; and if the debt was ever sold without a representation or warranty of accuracy. The plaintiff must attach specified documentation demonstrating the existence of the consumer debt, depending on the type of debt or obligation. Documentation evidencing the consumer debt must satisfy the authentication requirements of the court rule and statute governing business records as evidence. Notice Requirements . A consumer notice, in no less than 10 point type, must accompany the complaint or amended complaint warning that a default judgment may be awarded against the consumer. The notice must be substantially similar to a form set out in statute and must state a number of potential consequences that may occur if the consumer takes no action and a default judgment is entered against the consumer. The notice must also state that an attorney may provide assistance in understanding and responding to the action, and that the consumer may be able to obtain free or reduced fee legal services if the consumer is unable to afford an attorney. Failure to Comply . If a plaintiff fails to comply with the complaint and consumer notice requirements, the court may deny the plaintiff's motion for default judgment and may issue a notice that the court will dismiss the case without prejudice unless the plaintiff files an amended complaint that complies with the requirements within 30 days of the notice.
