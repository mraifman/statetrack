What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Sentencing Options for Survivors of Domestic Violence, Sexual Assault, or Human Trafficking . A sentencing alternative is established for eligible defendants who are survivors of domestic violence, sexual assault, or human trafficking, and the court is authorized to impose the sentencing alternative or a sentence below the standard range in applicable cases. A defendant is eligible for the sentencing alternative or other reduced sentence if: at the time of the offense, the defendant was a verified victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or human trafficking involving a pattern of severe or coercive abuse that created ongoing fear, control, or dependency, and subjected the defendant to substantial physical, sexual, or psychological abuse; the domestic violence or abuse suffered by the defendant was the primary and proximate cause of the defendant's criminal conduct; and generally applicable sentencing requirements would be unduly harsh given the nature and circumstances of the crime and the defendant's history, character, and condition. A verified victim is a person for whom there exists a criminal or civil court record referencing the person's victimization, a documented report from a qualified professional attesting to the person's victimization, or a protection order, restraining order, or no-contact order naming the person as a victim. Domestic violence or abuse suffered by a defendant may only be considered the primary and proximate cause of the defendant's criminal conduct if the court finds a temporal nexus between the domestic violence or abuse and the criminal conduct. Remote or historical domestic violence or abuse suffered by the defendant without a contemporaneous connection to the criminal conduct does not satisfy this requirement. Before imposing the sentencing alternative or other reduced sentence, the court must find whether the defendant meets the above eligibility criteria. The court may consider the following factors and evidence when making its finding: whether the underlying crime was committed against an intimate partner who committed domestic violence against the defendant; whether the defendant has been diagnosed with or treated for any behavioral health conditions related to prior victimization; evidence that the defendant's prior victimization affected the defendant's perception of safety and security, perception of the ability to receive help through law enforcement or other government entities, or ability to achieve financial independence; oral and written arguments, written statements, testimony, medical records and documents, physical evidence, specified protection orders, police reports, and recordings of 911 calls; and any other factors or evidence the court deems relevant. If the court finds that the defendant meets the eligibility criteria, the court may impose the sentencing alternative or other reduced sentence, which may include departing downward from any sentencing enhancements or imposing a sentence below any mandatory minimum terms, except for offenses involving sexual abuse. The court must impose the sentencing alternative if the defendant meets the eligibility criteria and the court finds the sentencing alternative is appropriate. The court may order the Department of Corrections (DOC) to complete a risk assessment or chemical dependency screening report to help determine whether the sentencing alternative is appropriate.
