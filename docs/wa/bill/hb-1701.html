HB 1701 · Liquor licensee premises

Allows liquor licensees and other businesses to operate separate premises in a shared facility, including leasing space and kitchens, with conditions.

What the bill does (WA Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

It is specified that nothing in Washington liquor laws prohibits an applicant for a liquor license from becoming licensed to operate out of a premises located on the property of a facility that includes separate premises of other liquor licensees or businesses. The owner of the facility may be, but is not required to be, one of the licensees operating out of a premises at the facility. A licensee who owns real property, or real and personal property, may lease that property to other licensees or businesses and multiple licensees or businesses may operate their own premises at the facility. A licensee may also lease use of its kitchen space to another person. The LCB may not consider the existence of these types of arrangements alone to impact a licensee's ownership and control of their premises or otherwise prevent licensure. Each licensee or business must operate out of their own premises on the facility property, except as otherwise authorized in current law for shared use of a consumption space, or under the bill for leased use of kitchen space. A licensee that leases use of its kitchen to another person must ensure that the other person holds all required permits, approvals, and licenses, including kitchen and food service permits required by the applicable local health department and state and local business licenses, before operating out of the kitchen. Leasing agreements may not include any profit or revenue sharing or require that the lessee serve products made by the lessor. The LCB may require submission of any lease agreement, operating agreement, and other contract in existence between licensees, businesses, and the owner of the facility, and between the different licensees and businesses at the facility, to ensure each licensee retains ownership and control over their licensed premises on the facility property. It is provided that nothing in the bill: (1) authorizes a licensee to engage in privileges not authorized under the terms of their license; (2) requires a licensee to obtain a liquor license for a type of activity conducted by another licensee from that other licensee's separate premises on the facility property; (3) may be construed to violate the prohibition on money advances by industry members to retailers or other provisions of Washington liquor laws; (4) authorizes a person who does not hold a liquor license to engage in any activity that requires holding a liquor license; or (5) authorizes a person, including a lessee of a kitchen, to engage in food preparation or service without holding all required permits, approvals, and licenses, including kitchen and food service permits required by the applicable local health department and state and local business licenses. It is also specified that nothing in the prohibitions on direct or indirect financial interests between industry members, affiliates, and retailers prohibits a person holding a domestic brewery license, microbrewery license, domestic winery license, distillery license, craft distillery license, or manufacturer's license from operating a premises in a facility at which a person holding a retail liquor license also operates in a separate space. The facility may be owned by any of the licensees and space may be leased to other licensees.
