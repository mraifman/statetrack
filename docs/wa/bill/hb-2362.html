HB 2362 · Impaired driving

Lowers the blood alcohol limit for impaired driving and related offenses, license sanctions, and probation conditions from 0.08 to 0.05, with a public campaign and WSIPP evaluation.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Impaired Driving Offenses . The breath or blood alcohol concentration limit of 0.08 percent or higher is reduced to 0.05 percent or higher for the offenses of Driving While Under the Influence and Actual Physical Control of a Motor Vehicle While Under the Influence, and for the unlawful behaviors of unlawful operation of a vessel in a reckless manner and reckless or impaired driving under the Washington Code of Military Justice. Blood and Breath Tests . An alcohol concentration of less than 0.08 is reduced to 0.05 as evidence that may be considered in determining whether the person was under the influence of intoxicating liquor. The liquid simulator solution external standard or dry gas external standard result is reduced to 0.045 to 0.055 inclusive. Driver's License Suspension . The alcohol concentration of 0.08 is reduced to 0.05 for the license sanctions and disqualifications imposed by the DOL and suspension, revocation, or denial of a driver's license, permit, or privilege following a breath test for purposes of the officer's warning, written notice after arrest, sworn report, and scope of the hearing. Criminal Penalty Probation Conditions . The alcohol concentration limit of 0.08 or more is reduced to 0.05 or more for the probation conditions imposed by a court. Unlawful Sale and Purchase of Liquor . The reduction in the breath or blood alcohol concentration from 0.08 to 0.05 does not change civil law for purposes of civil liability of a licensed commercial or quasi-commercial vendor. Public Information Campaign . The Washington Traffic Safety Commission (Commission) is required to develop and implement a public information campaign related to the reduction in the breath or blood alcohol concentration from 0.08 to 0.05. The Commission is required to ensure media coverage statewide, advertise in multiple issues in each county's largest newspaper, provide content in the top nine non-English languages, consider equity outcomes on overburdened communities, and collaborate with the hospitality industry to educate drivers by developing at least 10 percent of the advertisements with the industry. Washington State Institute for Public Policy Evaluation . The Washington State Institute for Public Policy is required to evaluate the impacts of the reduction in the breath or blood alcohol concentration from 0.08 to 0.05 and submit a report by March 1, 2028. The evaluation is required to include the impact of the reduction on serious and fatal traffic crashes, arrests and adjudications for Driving Under the Influence offenses, equity outcomes on overburdened communities, and business effects on the hospitality industry, breweries, wineries, and distilleries.
