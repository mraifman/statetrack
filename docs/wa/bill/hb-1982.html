HB 1982 · Treaty rights/convictions

Expands when people with treaty Indian fishing, hunting, gathering or pasturing convictions can vacate their records, and creates OPD representation and a tribal liaison counsel position.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Vacating a Record of Conviction . The circumstances where a person is eligible to apply to the sentencing court for vacation of the applicant's record of conviction for a misdemeanor, gross misdemeanor, or felony offense, based on the person's exercise of treaty Indian fishing rights, are expanded to authorize vacation of convictions: based on the exercise of treaty Indian fishing, hunting, gathering, or pasturing rights, rather than only fishing rights; regardless of when the conviction occurred; and including convictions that were based on a violation of an enjoined statute, ordinance, or rule that interfered with a treaty Indian right as determined under an applicable state supreme court or federal court decision, rather than only convictions based on a violation of an enjoined statute or rule. With respect to vacations based on the exercise of treaty Indian hunting rights, the court must also vacate the record of conviction if the applicant is a member of a tribe that may exercise treaty Indian hunting rights at the location where the offense occurred and: the state or local government has been enjoined from taking enforcement action of the statute, rule, or ordinance to the extent that it interferes with a treaty Indian hunting right as determined by an applicable state supreme court or federal court decision; the applicant's claim or defense based on the exercise of a treaty Indian hunting right was previously denied on the basis of case law that has since been repudiated; or the court finds that the applicant's exercise of a treaty Indian hunting right would have more likely than not been a defense to the offense. Office of Public Defense . The Office of Public Defense's (OPD's) employees are authorized to provide direct representation and consultation services to a person or, if the person is deceased, a family member or an official tribal representative acting on the person's behalf, for purposes of: filing motions to vacate convictions based on the exercise of treaty Indian rights; and appearing at related legal proceedings. Subject to appropriations, the OPD must provide access to direct representation and consultation services to persons eligible to vacate convictions based on the exercise of treaty Indian rights, and establish a position for a staff tribal liaison counsel. The staff tribal liaison counsel's responsibilities must include: performing outreach to tribal governments to identify and support tribal members with convictions related to the regulation of fishing, hunting, gathering, or pasturing activities that may be eligible for vacation; developing and disseminating educational material and information to tribal governments and their members about the process for vacating applicable convictions, and the services offered by the OPD; and providing direct representation and consultation services to persons eligible to vacate convictions based on the exercise of treaty Indian rights.
