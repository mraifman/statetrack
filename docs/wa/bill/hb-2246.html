What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

Extending Application of Imitation Firearm Violation . Charter schools, state-tribal education compact schools (STECs), the School for the Blind (WSSB), the Center for Deaf and Hard of Hearing Youth (CDHY), and educational service districts (ESDs)—except when the ESD is acting in its capacity as an institutional education provider—may suspend or expel an elementary or secondary student (student) for up to one year if the student acts with malice and displays an instrument that appears to be a firearm on public elementary or secondary school premises. Public Premises Definition . For purposes of firearm-related violations, "public elementary or secondary school premises" means any premises, transportation, or facility areas owned, operated, or used exclusively by a school district, charter school, STEC, the WSSB, the CDHY, or an ESD—except when the ESD is acting in its capacity as an institutional education provider. Return to School, Readmission, and Extracurricular Participation . For firearm-related violations, a school district may prohibit or limit a disciplined student's return to the school or educational setting from which the student was suspended or expelled. In contrast, the following entities may deny readmission to a student disciplined for a firearm-related violation: charter schools, STECs, the WSSB, the CDHY, and ESDs—except when the ESD is acting in its capacity as an institutional education provider. The school district, ESD, or other school that disciplined the student may also prohibit or limit the student's participation in, or attendance at, extracurricular or other activities. Governing Board Decision and Reconsideration . For students disciplined for firearm-related violations, a decision to prohibit or limit a student's return, or to deny readmission, must be made by the school district board of directors or other applicable governing board upon recommendation of the superintendent or other school administrator. The student's parents or guardians may request reconsideration by the board. The board may grant the request for reconsideration, but any decision regarding the granting of reconsideration, or made following reconsideration, is final. Reengagement Meetings and Reengagement Plans . For students disciplined for firearm-related violations, the reengagement meeting must include a discussion about whether the student will be reenrolled full time at the school or educational setting from which the student was suspended or expelled, or provided comparable services in another setting. The reengagement plan must be reevaluated with the student and the student's parents or guardians every six months until the student is reenrolled full time at the educational setting from which the student was suspended or expelled.
