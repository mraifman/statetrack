What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Orig.) (Summary of Bill))

The bill as referred to committee not considered. Summary of Bill (Proposed Substitute): Workforce Education Investment Account Funds for Degrees in Advanced Computing and Related Fields. Beginning in fiscal year 2025, 10 percent of the annual growth from advanced computing surcharge revenues must be for programs that lead to a credential in advanced computing and related science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM) computing fields to increase access and equity, especially for students from traditionally underserved communities. When sufficient student and employer demand is demonstrated, and the revenue growth is sufficient to fund high demand computing STEM programs, 100 additional annual degrees from the Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science and Engineering (Allen School) at the University of Washington (UW) must automatically be funded, adjusted annually for inflation. If the revenues to support the advanced and STEM computing degrees is less than sufficient, the amount provided for the degrees at the Allen School must be adjusted appropriately. Beginning June 30, 2026, UW must report to the appropriate committees of the Legislature on the program annually. Workforce Education Investment Account and Oversight Board. WSAC must provide WEIAOB and the higher education committees of the Legislature with a report by August 1st of each year that details: the expenditures from the Account; and the amount and the outcome of expenditures for programs that lead to a credential in advanced computing, and related STEM fields. The date that recommendations must be made from the Board to the Legislature every year is changed from August 1st to December 31st. Washington College Grant for Apprenticeships. Amount . Beginning in the 2025-26 academic year, students attending approved apprenticeship programs may receive the WCG-A for the tuition and fees the apprentice actually pays for RSI, not to exceed the maximum WCG for students attending two-year institutions of higher education. Grant for Associated Program Costs. All students attending approved apprenticeship programs who qualify for the maximum WCG may also receive a grant of up to $500 for associated program costs, which include program supplies and equipment. The amount in determining the cost to the apprentice cannot include tuition, fees, and associated program costs that the apprenticeship program would otherwise pay for. WSAC shall assess compliance with this requirement based on apprenticeship programs as they existed as of July 1, 2022. Academic Credit for Related Supplemental Institution. All public institutions of higher education must establish a policy for granting as many credits as possible and appropriate for RSI in active state apprenticeship programs. Credits must be approved within a certain amount of time, not to exceed one year from the date of the program's registration or the effective date of this act. Exceptions may be made for good cause on a case-by-case basis. All credits granted for RSI must count towards a degree as of July 1, 2026. The institution which granted the credit need not be the same as the institution where the apprentice receives the degree. WSAC must ensure that the statewide transfer of credit policy and agreement is designed to facilitate the transfer of credit and the evaluation of transcripts for students attending approved apprenticeship programs.
