What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Notice Requirements to Employees . An employer must provide written notice to an employee if the employer uses, or intends to use, electronic monitoring to assist the employer in conducting performance evaluations of the employee. If the employee is represented by a union, notice must also be provided to the employee's exclusive bargaining representative. For new hires, the notice must be provided at the time the job offer is made. For employers already using electronic monitoring for performance evaluation purposes, written notice must be provided within 30 days of the effective date of the act. For employers not yet using electronic monitoring for performance evaluation purposes, written notice must be provided at least 30 days prior to implementing the use of electronic monitoring. An employer is not prohibited from implementing electronic monitoring prior to providing the required notice if: the implementation of technology is necessary: (1) to prevent imminent harm to an employee or any person; (2) to prevent imminent theft, property damage, or other economic harm to the employer; or (3) in response to an emergency event involving the employer or to a state of emergency; and the employer notifies the employee as soon as practicable and follows up with a written notice within 30 days. The notice must include a statement saying that the employer uses technology to electronically monitor its employees. If the employer uses computer monitoring software, keystroke tracking, cameras or other video or photographic technology, audio recording or listening devices, artificial intelligence systems, or Global Positioning System (GPS) or other positioning technology for the primary purpose of monitoring for job performance, the employer must include a general list of the type of technology being used. The notice must also include a statement saying whether the employer has a verification process that includes meaningful human review to confirm data gathered by the electronic monitoring. Law enforcement agencies and firefighting agencies are not considered "employers" and therefore are excluded from the bill. Violations of Notice Requirements . An employee may file a complaint with the Department of Labor and Industries (Department) for violations of the requirement. If the Department investigates, it may issue either a citation and notice of assessment if the employer violated the notice requirement or a closure letter if the employee's allegation cannot be substantiated. A citation and notice of assessment must order the employer to comply with the notice requirements within 30 days, and if the employer complies, the Department may not issue civil penalties. If the employer does not comply with the order within 30 days, the Department may determine that the violation was willful and must order the employer to pay a civil penalty of $100 to $5,000. For repeat willful violations, the civil penalty can be no less than $5,000. The Department may waive or reduce civil penalties at any time. An employee may also bring a private cause of action against the employer for failing to provide the required notice. A prevailing employee is entitled to statutory damages of $100 to $5,000, plus other relief such as actual damages, reinstatement, injunctive relief, and other appropriate remedies.
