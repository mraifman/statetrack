HB 1460 · Protection order hope cards

Expands the Hope Card program for protection order petitioners, removes the fee, adds trauma-informed design, and adds firearm prohibition information to the card.

What the bill does (WA Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The list of entities the AOC must collaborate with in the development and implementation of the Hope Card program is expanded to include the Washington Supreme Court Gender and Justice Commission and representatives from gender-based violence survivor advocacy and legal assistance organizations. Card design and program implementation must use a trauma-informed approach and prioritize protection from harm. A person may request a Hope Card from the clerk of the court at the time the protection order is entered and from the AOC after entry of the protection order. A person who has been issued an extreme risk protection order may request a Hope Card. A person requesting a Hope Card may not be charged a fee. A Hope Card must be in a scannable format containing specified information if the clerk of the court or the AOC has the means and information available. Information to be included in a Hope Card is revised by removing the requirement for a physical description of the respondent and details about the order, and adding a requirement to include information on any court orders prohibiting the restrained person from accessing, having custody or control, possessing, purchasing, receiving, or attempting to purchase or receive firearms, other dangerous weapons, or a concealed pistol license. Where it is possible to include a digital record of the protection order in a scannable Hope Card, the following additional information must be included: petition attachments; protections and restraints ordered, including any firearm prohibitions; proof of compliance with any order to relinquish firearms; and violations of the order. The AOC must ensure that the information required to be in a Hope Card is provided by each court, including through use of consistent court codes, reporting mechanisms, and database entry.
