What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Funds from the Student Achievement Council Tuition Recovery Trust Fund are payable to the state for any consumer protection requirement for institutions of higher education. Money may be used for providing refunds to students affected by an institution's violation of any established consumer protection requirement. An institution of higher education that discontinues operations or that discontinues the educational program of any currently enrolled student must: provide a teach-out opportunity that allows the student to complete the program under the same material conditions as existed prior to the decision to discontinue the program, including in-person or distance education delivery, pedagogy, curricular content, class size, or other elements of program delivery described in recruitment materials; or enter into a transfer agreement acceptable to the WSAC that allows the student to complete the program under the same material conditions that existed prior to the decision to discontinue the program, including in-person or distance education delivery, pedagogy, curricular content, class size, or other elements of program delivery described in recruitment materials. The transfer agreement may not result in increased costs to students and must be with an institution that: is accredited by an accrediting association recognized by the WSAC and is in good standing with its accreditor; is not currently under financial aid restrictions by the United States Department of Education; provides the student a reasonable opportunity to complete the program with substantially the same number of credit hours as was required by the institution operating the closing program; is within a reasonable distance of the closing institution, unless instruction occurs entirely via distance education; and within the previous five years, has not had any judgement related to consumer protection law entered against it in favor of a law enforcement agency or entered into any settlement agreements related to a consumer protection law with a law enforcement agency. The WSAC is required to adopt rules to determine the time period and amount for which tuition and fees will be reimbursed to students. An institution that violates these provisions must be required to provide a refund of tuition and fees paid by a student who is enrolled at the time the student's program is closed. Institutional debt owed by a student who is enrolled in a program that is discontinued in violation of these provisions is void and may not be recovered, collected, or enforced by the institutions or any other person or entity. The consumer protections do not apply to a degree granting institution that: is a private, nonprofit education institution, the main campus of which is permanently situated in the state, which is open to residents of the state, which neither restricts entry on racial or religious grounds, which provides programs of education beyond high school leading at least to the baccalaureate degree, and which is accredited by the Northwest Association of Schools and Colleges or by an accrediting association recognized by the council for higher education; is authorized to participate in state financial aid programs; and was founded on or before 1910 or is an institution within the boundaries of a federally recognized tribal government and a minority-serving institution.
