SB 5945 · Persistent offenders

Requires resentencing for persistent offenders whose strike convictions occurred before age 18, with victim notice and statement rights; excludes murder and most serious sex offenses.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Persistent offender is defined to include convictions for murder in the first degree, murder in the second degree, or any sex offense considered a most serious offense at any age, or most serious offenses in which the person was 18 years of age or older at the time of the offense date for three strikes. A resentencing hearing is required in any case that the person has been sentenced as a persistent offender, if one of the convictions occurred with an offense date before the person turned 18 years old. A resentencing hearing is not required for a person that was convicted of murder in the first degree, murder in the second degree, or any sex offense considered a most serious offense, at any age. The prosecuting attorney for each county must review each sentencing document for any person sentenced as a persistent offender. The prosecutor must, or the person that was sentenced as a persistent offender with a conviction that occurred for an offense while under the age of 18 may, make a motion for relief from sentence to the original sentencing court. The prosecutor must make reasonable efforts to notify victims and survivors of victims of the motion for resentencing and the date of the resentencing hearing. Victim advocates must be made available to the victim or their survivors, and the court must provide an opportunity to the victim or their survivors to present a statement personally or through representation. All notice requirements must comply with the requirements under state law. The sentencing court shall grant the motion if it finds that a conviction for an offense that was committed when the person was under the age of 18 was used as a basis for a finding that the person was a persistent offender, unless the person was convicted of murder in the first degree, murder in the second degree, or any sex offense considered a most serious offense. The court must set an expedited resentencing date. At resentencing, not withstanding the requirement that any sentence imposed be determined by the law in effect at the time of the offense, for purposes of resentencing, any conviction for an offense under the age of 18 must be sentenced as if it were not a most serious offense, regardless of if the offense happened before, on, or after the 2019 law that removed robbery in the second degree from qualifying as a strike offense. Applies retroactively to persons incarcerated in total confinement as persistent offenders on the effective date of the bill, regardless of the date of offense or conviction that resulted in the person being sentenced as a persistent offender. EFFECT OF CHANGES MADE BY HUMAN SERVICES COMMITTEE (First Substitute): Requires victims and survivors of victims of any crimes committed by the offender to be notified of the motion for resentencing and the date of the resentencing hearing. Permits victims and survivors to present a statement either personally or by representation. Requires the prosecuting attorney and court to comply with chapter 7.69 RCW. Includes persons convicted of murder in the first degree, murder in the second degree, or any sex offense considered a most serious offense at any age as persistent offenders. Restores the two-strikes definition of persistent offender. Resentencing is not necessary for any person that has been convicted of murder in the first or second degree, or any sex offense considered a most serious offense.
