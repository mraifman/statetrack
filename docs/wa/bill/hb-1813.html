Expands behavioral health crisis services by requiring MCO-BHASO crisis delegation, Medicaid rate adjustments for service changes, and coordinated crisis hotline and care coordination standards.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Delivery of Behavioral Health Crisis Services . The Health Care Authority (Authority) must consult with the Department of Commerce and the Department of Health on a quarterly basis to plan and prepare for new or expanded services in each regional service area, including incorporating regional capacity changes reported to the Authority. When programs or facilities are newly established or closed or existing services are expanded or reduced in a region, the Authority must direct the state's Medicaid contractor for actuarial services to adjust Medicaid managed care rates to account for the new, expanded, or reduced service or the facility closure. For new or expanded services, the adjustment must be performed prior to the facility opening or the service expansion. The Authority must direct managed care organizations (MCOs) to establish, continue, or expand delegation arrangements with behavioral health administrative services organizations (BHASOs) for crisis services for managed care enrollees. The services may include crisis phone interventions, mobile crisis teams, peer support services in crisis settings, and crisis stabilization services, such as crisis stabilization facilities, in-home crisis stabilization services, and crisis relief centers. The MCOs must negotiate with BHASOs on a structure to reimburse delegated network providers for medical services provided at crisis facilities. The MCOs must maintain delegation standards that are consistent with those of the National Committee for Quality Assurance. If a BHASO is not able to meet delegation standards for facility-based crisis stabilization services, the Authority and the MCO may provide technical assistance to the BHASO. If the Authority finds that the BHASO is unable to comply with the delegation standards at the end of the technical assistance period, the Authority may require that the delegation for facility-based crisis stabilization services be terminated and the responsibility for providing the services revert to the MCO. In addition, BHASOs are subject to audits of their performance with respect to the quality of services provided to enrollees. The MCOs and BHASOs must collectively establish defined roles, responsibilities, and protocols for care coordination for managed care enrollees with engagement with the crisis system of care. The scope of the behavioral health crisis hotlines operated by the BHASOs is clarified to require that they provide immediate support, triage, and referral, including tribal and Indian health care provider crisis services, for persons experiencing behavioral health crises. The services must include the capacity to connect persons with crisis counselors and dispatch additional crisis services consistent with existing strategies and operations of the 988 system. The general responsibility of the BHASOs to contract with enough providers for crisis services is clarified to include crisis services delegated from an MCO. The BHASOs must collaborate with the Authority to develop a funding model to determine adequate reserve thresholds with consideration of service utilization, crisis system operations, and crisis service needs. By January 1, 2026, the BHASOs, in consultation with MCOs, must develop and implement electronic care coordination data sharing standards that are consistent across regional service areas.
