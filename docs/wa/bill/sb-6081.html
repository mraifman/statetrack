SB 6081 · Government record privacy

Restricts state agencies from disclosing or displaying sex designation change records, removes sex-change indicators from credentials, and expands public records exemptions.

What the bill does (WA Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Department of Licensing Records . The DOL is prohibited from displaying, transmitting, or otherwise making available any record or supporting documentation of a person's sex designation change unless the person provides consent for a specific purpose. Credentials and digital records issued after a change to a person's sex designation may not include any indicator that the person's sex designation has been changed, including credentials and digital records transmitted for purposes of voter registration and to WaTech in the DOL's annual transmission of its electronic data file on all drivers and identicard holders. Public Records Act Exemption. Exemptions under the PRA relating to driver's license and permit records are expanded to include any record or information used to prove sex designation when applying for, renewing, or making changes to a driver's license, instruction permit, or identicard. Further, any record pertaining to a confidential vehicle license plate, driver's license, or identicard that, alone or in combination with any other records, may reveal the sex designation of an individual performing an undercover or covert law enforcement, confidential public health work, public assistance fraud, or child support investigative activity, is exempt from disclosure under the PRA. Disclosure of Records to Another Jurisdiction . Records and supporting documents contained in a person's Washington driver's record that include the person's sex designation change may not be shared with another jurisdiction that requests the driver's record unless the person gives consent for a specific purpose. Vital Records . Personal information collected from individuals who have requested a change to their sex designation in a vital record is exempt from disclosure under the PRA. Supporting documentation related to sex designation changes must be sealed by the state registrar and may not be disclosed, and any supporting documentation transferred to the State Archives related to sex designation changes may not be made publicly available. The reasons for which the DOH may deny a request for vital statistics data or records are expanded to include a determination by the DOH that there is a reasonable possibility of the data being used to cause or enable harm or disenfranchisement; that its use could reasonably result in disparate impact on the data subject; or that otherwise fulfilling the request would not be in the public interest. The DOH is prohibited from releasing a certification of birth to any government agency or court for the same reasons. Except for certifications and informational copies of vital records, all vital records, reports, supporting documentation, vital statistics, data, and information contained in these records that are maintained by the DOH are confidential and not subject to state or federal discovery, subpoenas, or other compulsory process.
