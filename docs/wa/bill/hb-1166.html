What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2025 (Summary of Bill))

The Providing Effective Education for Reentry Success Act (Act) is established within the Department of Corrections (DOC). The DOC must allow incarcerated individuals to purchase and retain laptops using current technology as personal property. Subject to appropriations, amounts available in the Incarcerated Individual Betterment Fund, and funds from grants and donations, the DOC must provide incarcerated individuals who possess laptops access to Wi-Fi at each correctional facility. Funds received by the DOC on behalf of an incarcerated individual for purchasing a laptop, associated accessories, or technology, or for costs associated with pursuing online learning, including tuition, fees, electronic books and media, and online subscription services are not subject to mandatory deductions. The DOC must collaborate with the Governor's Statewide Broadband Office to ensure incarcerated individuals have meaningful opportunities for digital inclusion and digital equity, and make websites of educational value available to incarcerated individuals within two weeks of a request for access. The DOC must ensure that incarcerated individuals who use their online access privileges responsibly to pursue educational goals do not have access limited due to concerns about misuse committed by others. The DOC must approve as prison education programs distance education programs and correspondence courses leading to an associate, bachelor, or graduate degree, or a direct transfer associate degree. The DOC must collaborate with nonprofits and community-based postsecondary education programs to create and expand postsecondary degrees and certificate programs. Participating nonprofits and community-based postsecondary education programs must provide accredited postsecondary education degree or certificate programs to incarcerated individuals—or faculty, graduate or undergraduate students, or community professionals in correctional facilities to support postsecondary learning and professional development through study groups, lectures, seminars, peer-to-peer and peer-led study, and other activities. The DOC must provide approved nonprofits and community-based postsecondary education programs with at least one room in each correctional facility for incarcerated individuals and the staff, volunteers, and sponsors of such nonprofits and programs to maintain and use technology, including smart boards, computers with internet access, photocopiers, and printers, for fulfilling educational goals. The DOC may approve health-related digital technologies for incarcerated individuals to purchase as personal property. Correspondence courses leading to an associate, bachelor, or graduate degree are eligible for state and federal financial aid. Application of the DOC priority criteria for selection of an incarcerated individual to participate in state-funded postsecondary degree or certificate education programs is limited to no more than 67 percent of available seats, except pre-apprenticeship programs. The DOC priority criteria is also modified to consider the following conditions in the order listed: incarcerated individuals who have completed a prerequisite for a state-funded postsecondary degree or certificate education programs, in priority of time since completion: 0 to 5 years, 5 to 10 years, and 10 or more years; and whether an individual already possesses a degree or has an individual reentry plan that includes participation in a postsecondary degree or certificat
