HB 2445 · Probate

Tightens who may serve as estate personal representative, adds petition and bond requirements, and sets venue rules for suitable-person appointments.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Who May Serve as a Personal Representative to Administer an Estate . The time period for a person entitled to administer an estate to petition for letters of administration is extended. The court must allow at least 60 days, rather than 40 days, to have elapsed since death before appointing a public guardian or guardian ad litem to administer the estate. The court must allow at least 90 days, rather than 40 days, to have elapsed before appointing a "suitable person." Unless a person appointed under the "suitable person" provisions is a trust company, bank, or a professional service corporation formed by attorneys, such person is limited to two petitions for appointment per year. The following are added to the list of those not qualified to act as personal representatives: a person who within the last 24 months has had letters of administration revoked for cause, such as waste, embezzlement, or fraud on the estate; a person who has been found by a court or administrative agency within the last 36 months to engage in acts of dishonesty, theft, or breach of fiduciary duty; and a person who seeks appointment under the "suitable person" provisions and who is a principal or agent of or acting in concert with any person or entity likely to be involved in the sale, purchase, or transfer of a major probate asset. The court must revoke the letters of administration issued to a "suitable person" who is subsequently found to be a principal or agent of a person or entity involved in the sale, purchase, or transfer or a major probate asset. The court may also impose sanctions against such person. Venue in Probate Proceedings . If a petitioner seeks appointment to administer an estate under the "suitable person" provisions, the venue for all probate proceedings must be the Washington county where the decedent resided at the time of death or in which any part of the probate estate may be, if the decedent was not a Washington resident at the time of death. Application for Letters of Administration, Notice of Appointment, and Bond Requirement . A petition for letters of administration must be verified by oath of the applicant and must state that the applicant is permitted by statute to administer the estate and is not disqualified from acting as a personal representative for any reason, including because of a conviction of any felony or crime involving moral turpitude. A petition for letters of administration must state the basis of the applicant's knowledge of the fact that the decedent died without a will and provide details of the applicant's reasonable search for any known heirs. Unless the applicant is the surviving spouse or next-of-kin, the petition must also set forth a general description of the major probate assets of the estate, including real property, motor vehicles, and any property or interest estimated to exceed $10,000 in value. In addition to providing notice of the personal representative's appointment, the personal representative must file with the clerk of the court a report confirming notice to third parties no later than 30 days after appointment. The report must set forth the names and addresses of each heir and other third parties to which notice was provided, the details of the personal representative's search to identify such persons, and the date and manner in which notice was provided.
