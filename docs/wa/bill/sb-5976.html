SB 5976 · Commercial electronic mail

Narrows the Commercial Electronic Mail Act and repeals several per se Consumer Protection Act violations for commercial email, limiting liability and damage claims.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Orig.) (Summary of Bill))

Narrowing the Application of the Commercial Electronic Mail Act. A sender must have a reliable basis, rather than reason, to know that an email is held by a Washington resident for the sender to be in violation of the Act. A commercial email's subject line must be likely to mislead a recipient, acting reasonably, about a fact material to the relevant transaction and be material to the recipient completing the transaction. A recipient of a commercial email in violation of the Act may only claim damages if they received, reviewed, and detrimentally relied upon the email, rather than just received the email. Repealing Specified Per Se Violations of the Consumer Protection Act. The following per se CPA violations are repealed: initiating or conspiring with another person to initiate the transmission of a commercial email that: uses a third party internet domain name without the third party's consent; misrepresents or obscures any information in identifying the point of origin or transmission path of the email; or contains false or misleading information in the subject line; and assisting in the transmission of a commercial email when the person knows, or consciously avoids knowing, that the initiator of the commercial email is engaged, or intends to engage, in any act or practice that violates the CPA. Lawsuits filed on or after the effective date are subject to the provisions, regardless of when the conduct occurred.
