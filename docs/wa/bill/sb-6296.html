SB 6296 · Involuntary treatment

Expands who can petition for involuntary detention, creates a presumption against voluntary treatment, requires officers to assist detentions, and limits ambulance restraint use.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Court Consultation of Judicial Information System. Before granting relief under the ITA, a court must consult JIS or any related databases to determine the respondent's criminal history, prior civil commitments under the ITA, pendency of other proceedings involving the respondent, and the respondent’s firearms history. Ambulances. An ambulance service is authorized to transport a patient detained or committed for evaluation or treatment pursuant to a judicial warrant, custody order, or pickup order authorized by a DCR or officer. An ambulance service may not use restraints while transporting a patient detained or committed for evaluation or treatment unless: the patient is combative or interfering with the provision of medical care; there is reasonable cause to believe the patient will elope; or there is reasonable cause to believe restraints are necessary for the safety of patients or staff. If the ambulance service determines that restraints are necessary, it must use the least restrictive form of restraint possible. Non-Emergency Initial Detention. If a person a DCR is evaluating has a history of not following through with voluntary evaluation and treatment plans or prematurely discontinuing voluntary treatment, there is a presumption that the person will not voluntarily seek appropriate treatment in good faith. If the DCR decides not to detain the person because they have determined that the person will voluntarily seek appropriate treatment, the DCR will document the reasons for that determination. If a superior court judge issues a warrant to detain a person based on a DCR's request, an officer must provide assistance in detaining the person. In rare circumstances, an officer may decline to provide assistance if they determine based on documented evidence that the amount of force required to take the person into custody could cause greater harm than the risk of harm to the person and the community posed by the person's behavioral health condition. When an officer declines to give assistance, the officer must file a report with the health care authority and the region's behavioral health administrative services organization. Emergency Initial Detention. If a person is located in a 23-hour crisis relief center when a DCR takes the person into emergency custody, the person may remain at the crisis relief center or an adjacent crisis stabilization facility in accordance with Department of Health rules. Upon request of a DCR, a peace officer must provide assistance in detaining the person. In rare circumstances, the officer may decline to provide assistance if they make a determination based on documented evidence that the amount of force required to take the person into custody could cause greater harm than the risk of harm to the person and the community posed by the person's behavioral health condition. When an officer declines to provide assistance, the officer shall file a report with the authority and the region's behavioral health administrative services organization.
