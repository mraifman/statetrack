HB 2044 · Unexcused student absences

Requires schools to start truancy interventions after the second unexcused absence, sets attendance agreements through CEBs, and makes truancy petitions a later, sequential last resort.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Senate Bill Report (Orig.) (Summary of Bill))

School District Duties . For a child aged 8 through 17, a school district must take data-informed steps to reduce the child's unexcused absences between the second and fifth unexcused absence, rather than between the second and seventh unexcused absence. In addition to other data-informed steps that may be taken where appropriate, a school district may offer assistance in enrolling the child in the Washington National Guard Youth Challenge Program. Filing a truancy petition is no longer an alternative to entering into an attendance agreement; rather these approaches to reduce a child's unexcused absences must be attempted sequentially. After a child's fifth unexcused absence in any month, a school district is required to either: enter into an attendance agreement with the child and parent or, if the child is younger than age eight, enter into an agreement with the parent; or refer the child to a CEB, so that the CEB can enter into an attendance agreement with the child and parent or the child and the school district or, if the child is younger than age eight, the CEB must enter into an agreement with the parent and the school district. When a child transfers to another school district, any attendance agreement must be provided to the receiving school district. Community Engagement Boards . The CEB must meet with the child, a parent, and a representative of the school district within 20 days of the referral. A child under age eight must not be required to attend the meeting. For the purpose of CEBs, an attendance agreement describes the intervention and prevention efforts that will be made available to the child and the parent, sets forth expectations for substantially reducing the child's unexcused absences, and establishes a deadline for compliance. Truancy Petitions . The attendance agreement must be included in a truancy petition filed with the juvenile court. If the school district or the CEB fails to enter into an attendance agreement with the child and parent—or the parent only, if the child is younger than age eight—within 20 days of the initial meeting, then the school district is required to file a truancy petition with the juvenile court. In addition, if the parent or child—or the parent only, if the child is younger than age eight—fails to comply with the attendance agreement by the deadline set by the agreement, then the school district is required to file a truancy petition with the juvenile court. In either case, the truancy petition must be filed no later than the child's fifteenth unexcused absence during the school year. A school district may, at its discretion, file a truancy petition with the juvenile court if the parent or the child, or both—or parent only if the child is younger than age eight—has entered into and complied with an attendance agreement, but the interventions and prevention efforts failed to substantially reduce the child's unexcused absences by the deadline specified in the agreement. In this case, the truancy petition may be filed any time after the child's fifteenth unexcused absence in a school year. Juvenile courts are no longer required to stay a truancy petition to refer the child and parent to a CEB or other coordinated means of intervention, if available. Other coordinated interventions are added to the list of actions that a juvenile court can take in lieu of hearing a truancy petition, if it would substantially reduce the child's unexcused absences.
