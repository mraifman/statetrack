What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Suspended Disposition Alternatives . The court must grant a suspended disposition alternative (Option B or Option C) when a juvenile is eligible unless the court makes an independent finding supported by a preponderance of the evidence that commitment to juvenile rehabilitation is needed because remaining in the community on community supervision would not adequately protect the community for juvenile offenses with a standard range term of confinement over 30 days (except for Murder in the first degree and second degree, Homicide by Abuse, Manslaughter in the first and second degree, Assault in the first degree, Kidnapping in the first degree, Rape in the first degree, Assault of a Child in the first and second degree, violent offenses with certain criminal history, sex offenses, Hit and Run resulting in death, and Vehicular Homicide). In making the finding described above, the court must consider certain factors, including: the severity of the offense(s); the juvenile's criminal history; whether the programming offered in a juvenile facility is appropriate to meet the needs of the juvenile; whether the goals of rehabilitation and community safety can be met in a less restrictive environment; whether the goals of rehabilitation and community safety can be met in a less restrictive disposition that is available; and the juvenile's age, developmental maturity, mental and emotional health, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, and any disabilities or special needs impacting the safety or suitability of institutional commitment. The court may place juveniles on electronic monitoring for up to the minimum of a juvenile's standard range when the court does not make a finding described above. Suspended Disposition Alternative (Option B) . Eligibility for the Option B disposition alternative is expanded to include individuals adjudicated of Assault in the second degree, Robbery in the first degree and Attempted Robbery in the first degree, Unlawful Possession of a Firearm, certain felony offenses where there is an allegation that the juvenile was armed with a firearm during commission of the offense, and those with a prior Option B disposition alternative. Substance Use Disorder or Mental Health Disposition Alternative (Option C) . The eligibility for a substance use disorder or mental health disposition alternative, also known as "Option C," is expanded to all juveniles except those adjudicated for an offense that is excluded from the finding described above. In addition, individuals with certain prior criminal history are now eligible for this disposition alternative (current law prohibits individuals with a prior A-, B++, or B+ offense). Appeal of Juvenile Dispositions . Individuals may appeal juvenile dispositions with a term of confinement over 30 days, except for standard range dispositions that do not require the independent finding described above. The Court of Appeals must make certain findings to uphold a finding that commitment to juvenile rehabilitation is needed, as follows: that the reasons supplied by the disposition judge are supported by the record; and that those reasons by a preponderance of the evidence support the conclusion that commitment to juvenile rehabilitation is needed because a community-based placement would not adequately protect the community.
