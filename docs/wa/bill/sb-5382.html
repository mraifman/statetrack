SB 5382 · Ballot measure petitions

Requires petition circulators to sign expanded declarations and requires the Secretary of State to reject petition signatures whose residence address does not match voter registration records.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Requiring Petition Signature Gathers to Sign Declarations. Petition signature gatherers circulating initiative or referendum petitions must sign the declaration contained on each sheet of a petition as provided by law. Adding a Signature, Date, and Address Lines to Petition Declarations and Making Other Changes. The petition declarations for initiatives and referenda are changed so that the signature gatherer, under the penalty of false swearing, must also swear or affirm, to the best of their knowledge, the following additional elements: that the persons signing the petition have reviewed the sheet; and that in addition to not receiving compensation or the promise of compensation, the persons signing the petition have not received a gratuity or promise of gratuity. The following lines are added to petition declarations for the signature gatherer to fill: signature, date, address, and county. Requiring the Secretary of State to Use Residential Addresses When Verifying Petition Signatures. When verifying a signature on an initiative or referendum petition, the Secretary is required to verify that the residence address on the petition is the same as the residence address on the current or previous affidavit of voter registration. The Secretary must reject the signature if: there is no residential address match; the petition does not specify the residence address, or the information required to be provided under the laws governing initiatives and referenda is not contained in the petition. The Secretary may not invalidate a signature for minor discrepancies, such as an incomplete or inaccurate apartment or unit number in the signer's residence address. An invalidated signature does not affect the validity of another valid signature in the particular petition. The Secretary may still use statistical sampling techniques to verify signatures. Stating Legislative Findings. In order to guard against fraud and mistake, the Legislature finds that signature gatherers should be required to sign the petition declaration under penalty of false swearing, and that residence addresses of registered voters signing the petition should be verified. EFFECT OF CHANGES MADE BY STATE GOVERNMENT, TRIBAL AFFAIRS & ELECTIONS COMMITTEE (First Substitute): Adds the following findings: reemphasizes previous legislative findings, codified in law, disfavoring the paying of signature gatherers; notes that current circulator declarations regularly go unsigned, requiring clarification of the law; finds that verifying a petitioner’s residence address will improve the accuracy and integrity of signature matching and enable meaningful enforcement of laws addressing petition misconduct; finds that verifying residential addresses will improve the validation process that the secretary of state has previously used; and finds that verifying a petitioner's address is similar to requirements in other states, including Idaho and California. Aligns and simplifies several sections that require signature gatherers to sign declarations. Allows the secretary of state to verify a petitioner's residential address using an address listed on a previous affidavit of voter registration. Clarifies that the secretary of state must reject a signature if there is no residential address match.
