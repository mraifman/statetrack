SB 6062 · Criminal offenses under 18

Requires courts to grant suspended dispositions to eligible juveniles, expands eligible offenses, and lowers sentencing grid ranges for several robbery offenses.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Juvenile Sentencing. A court is required to grant a suspended disposition alternative under Option B or Option C when a juvenile is eligible, unless the court makes an independent finding supported by a preponderance of the evidence, that commitment to the department is needed because remaining in the community and being placed on community supervision with a suspended disposition, would not adequately protect the community. This applies to all juveniles that have a standard range term of confinement over 30 days. Excluded offenses from consideration of a suspended disposition alternative include, murder in the first degree and second degree, homicide by abuse, manslaughter in the first and second degree, assault in the first degree, kidnapping in the first degree, rape in the first degree, assault of a child in the first and second degree, violent offenses with certain criminal history, sex offenses, hit and run resulting in death, and vehicular homicide. When making the finding on whether commitment to a JR facility is appropriate as described above, the court must consider certain factors, including: the severity of the offense or offenses; the juvenile's criminal history; whether programming, treatment, and education offered and provided in a JR facility is appropriate to meet the needs of the juvenile; whether the goals of rehabilitation and community safety can be met by assigning the juvenile to a less restrictive disposition that is available to the court; the juveniles age, developmental maturity, mental and emotional health, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, and any disabilities or special needs impacting the safety or suitability of institutional commitment; and whether the juvenile and the community will benefit from use of this disposition alternative after considering any examinations ordered under this section and the victim's opinion regarding whether the juvenile should receive a suspended disposition under this section. The court is allowed to extend a suspended disposition for an additional one-year period for good cause. The court may also impose a determinate sentence of electronic monitoring if it is available in the jurisdiction, for up to the minimum of the juvenile's standard range. All conditions the juvenile must follow to maintain the suspended disposition must be in writing. Robbery in the first degree when committed at age 15 or under is reduced from A (103 to 129 weeks) to A- (30-40 weeks) on the grid. Robbery in the first degree committed at age 16 or 17 is moved from A++ (129 to 260 weeks) to A (103-129 weeks) and any attempt, conspiracy, bailjump, or solicitation is moved from A (103-129 weeks) to B+ (15-36 weeks). Robbery in the second degree is moved from B+ (15-36 weeks) to B (local sanctions) on the grid. Suspended Disposition Alternatives. Option B Suspended Disposition Alternative. Expands eligibility for juveniles to receive an Option B sentence for anyone adjudicated of robbery in the first degree or an attempt, conspiracy, or solicitation to commit robbery in the first degree, assault in the second degree, unlawful possession of a firearm, certain offenses involving a motor vehicle, certain felony offenses where there is any allegation that the juvenile was armed with a firearm during the commission of the offense, and those with a prior Option B disposition alternative in their juvenile history.
