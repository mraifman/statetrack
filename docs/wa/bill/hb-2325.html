HB 2325 · Tourism assessment

Authorizes a Tourism Assessment Program that levies a percentage of business gross revenue, governed by an expanded Authority board and a Ratepayer Oversight Board.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Tourism Assessment Program . The Authority is authorized to establish a Tourism Assessment Program (TAP), subject to review by the Director of Commerce, that is designed, proposed, and governed by the Ratepayer Oversight Board (ROB), described below. The Authority is required to levy and collect a percentage of a business's gross revenue as an annual assessment. The assessments will be collected on an annual basis, after ratification by the businesses in each industry sector. The membership of the Authority's Board is modified by expanding the number of members from 13 to 17, and specifying the non-legislative membership as follows: eight members must be nominated by the ROB and must represent businesses paying the assessments; two members must represent official state destination marketing organizations; two members must represent tourism businesses subject to the assessment; and one member must represent a trade association that represents businesses that are subject to the assessment. The Authority, with review of the Director of Commerce, is required to approve an annual budget for the TAP. The Authority is required to approve or reject the budget recommended by the ROB and may not amend, replace, or independently adopt policies or provisions not submitted by the ROB. The budget is required to include: a complete and detailed financial program of the ROB, showing its revenues and expenditures; an explanation describing the administration and implementation of the ROB's programs and priorities, and identifying reasons for changes from prior expenditures or revenue items; an explanation of changes to financial policies, including an outline of the proposed policies for the ensuing fiscal period and performance indicators that demonstrate measurable progress toward the ROB's priorities; and sufficient detail to provide transparency for the ROB's actions on behalf of the industry. A regular audit of the budget is required to be conducted by the State Auditor. The Authority is required to submit an annual report to the Legislature and the Governor. The Authority is required to adopt rules and give substantial weight to the recommendations in the October 31, 2025, report titled Tourism Self-Supported Assessment Advisory Group: Report to the Legislature and the ROB's proposal. The rules do not apply to federally recognized Indian tribes, businesses, or enterprises unless the tribe or tribal member has opted to participate in the TAP. The state and its agents are immune from liability for obligations incurred by the Authority or the ROB. Liabilities and claims against the Authority or ROB are only enforceable against the assets of the Authority or ROB as if it were a corporation. The liability of the members of the Authority and ROB are several, not joint, and no member shall be liable for the default of any other member. Ratepayer Oversight Board . The Authority is required to appoint an initial 10-member ROB. The Group is required to prepare a list of nominees for the initial ROB membership. The initial ROB members must include two representatives for each of the tourism business sectors: lodging, travel services, attractions, recreation, beverage producers, full-service restaurants, and retailer locations. An ROB nominee must be an employee or officer of a business proposed to be subject to the assessment. An ROB member may be removed by the Authority, with the review of the Director of Commerce, for inefficiency, neglect of duty, malfea
