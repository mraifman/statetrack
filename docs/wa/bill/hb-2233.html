HB 2233 · Retail bags

Restricts thin film plastic carryout bags, raises the paper bag pass-through charge to 20 cents, adds manufacturer penalties, and bars charges to WIC and benefit card users.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

Film plastic carryout bags with a thickness of up to 10 mil are restricted. The pass-through charge for paper carryout bags is increased from 8 cents to 20 cents. Restrictions on prohibited types of carryout bags apply to the sale, distribution or shipment of restricted bags manufacturers and distributors of carryout bags, in addition to the provision of such bags by retail establishments to customers. Penalties are established for violations by manufacturers and distributors of up to $2,000 for a first violation, up to $5,000 for a second violation, and up to $10,000 for a third and subsequent violations. Violations by retail establishments remain subject to penalties of up to $250. Retail establishments that use a third-party platform must list applicable pass-through charges associated with each customer order on the platform and must collect the pass-through charge from the customer through the platform. Handleless bags are not carryout bags subject to restrictions on film plastic or pass-through charges if they are used by consumers inside stores or by retail establishments within a delivered or picked-up carryout bag. Carryout paper bags may meet D8410, a second type of ASTM composting standard, in order to be deemed capable of composting and therefore eligible to be used as a paper carryout bags. Retail establishments are prohibited from collecting both pass-through charges and any remaining difference between the pass-through charge and the establishment's customary price for a paper carryout bag from anyone using a voucher or electronic benefits card issued through the WIC program or state administered cash and food assistance support programs. This prohibition applies regardless of whether the card was used for all or part of a transaction.
