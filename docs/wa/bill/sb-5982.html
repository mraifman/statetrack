SB 5982 · Consumer-owned utilities

Extends CETA clean-energy compliance to ports and self-generating market customers, adds UTC reporting and enforcement, and delays a consumer-owned utility report to 2030.

What the bill does (WA Substitute Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Amended Definitions. Under CETA, the definition of consumer-owned utility is expanded to include port districts (ports) engaged in the business of distributing electricity. A consumer-owned utility must have at least one, rather than more than one, retail electric customer in Washington. A consumer-owned utility does not include a PUD with only one customer, provided the customer remains the same as of January 1, 2026, and the PUD continues to receive 100 percent of its electricity from the Bonneville Power Administration. A market customer is redefined as a nonresidential consumer of electricity, rather than a customer of an electric utility, that purchases electricity from an entity that is not a utility subject to CETA; or generates electricity for its own consumption, its lessee’s consumption, or both. A consumer no longer has to generate enough electricity to meet 100 percent of its own needs to meet the definition of market customer. An affected market customer is also defined as an electricity consumer, rather than the customer of a utility. An affected market customer does not include a nonresidential customer that prior to May 7, 2019, generated electricity to meet less than 100 percent of its own needs continues to consume electricity with the same or greater percentage of CETA-compliance electricity, for the same end uses. It is clarified that, for electric utilities, retail electric load is the amount of megawatt hours of electricity delivered in a given calendar year to its customers. For an affected market customer, retail electric load is newly defined as the amount of megawatt-hours of electricity purchased from an entity that is not a utility subject to CETA, or self-generated for its own consumption. Reporting and Compliance for Affected Market Customers. The Utilities and Transportation Commission (UTC) must adopt rules to establish reporting requirements for affected market customers to demonstrate compliance with CETA. Reporting requirements must be consistent with current law to the extent practicable. The UTC may waive these reporting requirements for affected market customers that: only procure power from nonemitting electric generation or renewable resources; or generate electricity using de minimis amounts of fossil fuels exclusively for emergency backup requirements. The UTC must, on its own motion or if requested, enforce affected market customers' compliance with CETA. Commerce must establish reporting requirements for ports, but it may waive reporting requirements that do not pertain to customers served by ports. For ports, the consumer-owed utility interim performance report is due July 1, 2030, rather than July 1, 2026.
