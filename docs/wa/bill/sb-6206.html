What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Subject to appropriations, Commerce must establish a pilot program to incentivize and make child care more accessible to first responders. "First responders" means firefighters, law enforcement officers, public safety telecommunicators, emergency medical personnel, as licensed or certificated by this state, and human service professionals that respond to emergency situations involving behavioral health crises and people experiencing complex medical needs including, but not limited to, social workers, behavioral health clinicians, advanced practice registered nurses, registered nurses, community health workers, and peer support specialists. Commerce must award grants with funds appropriated to local jurisdictions to provide incentive payments to licensed or certified child care providers who care for children of paid and volunteer first responders. Incentive payments must be prioritized for licensed or certified child care providers that offer child care during nonstandard work hours, on short notice, and when a child has an illness. Commerce must select at least four local jurisdictions, which can be counties, cities or incorporated towns, with one in each of the north and south regions of Washington State and on each side of the Cascade mountains to receive state grants. Selected local jurisdictions must be able to provide a plan of how they can equally match state grants for incentive payments with nonstate resources. The plan may include future nonstate resources including a sales and use tax for criminal justice purposes. By November 1, 2029, Commerce must submit a report that includes the outcomes of this pilot program. At a minimum, the report must describe the impact on recruitment of personnel, personnel retention, and discernable job satisfaction, resulting from program benefits and accessibility. EFFECT OF CHANGES MADE BY EARLY LEARNING & K-12 EDUCATION COMMITTEE (First Substitute): Includes incentive payments for child care providers who care for children of first responders within the definition of criminal justice purposes for the 0.1 percent sales and use tax for criminal justice purposes enacted in 2025. Adds that first responders include paid and volunteer first responders. Specifies that local jurisdiction means a county, city, or incorporated town.
