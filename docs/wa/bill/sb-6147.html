SB 6147 · Grocery estab. closures

Requires large grocery closures in food deserts or low-income areas to give four months' notice, negotiate alternatives, and voids certain negative use restrictions.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Notice Requirements. Certain grocery establishments located in geographic areas designated as food deserts using United States Department of Agriculture criteria, or in certain low-income census tracts, must provide written notice at least four months before closing to the city council, county council or commission, local health department, state legislators representing the location of the grocery establishment, and Office of the Attorney General (AG). The grocery establishment must also provide notice of its intention to close to its customers and the public by posting prominent written notices at all public entries and exits to the grocery establishment. Good Faith Engagement. During the closure notice period, the grocery establishment must, upon reasonable request, meet and work in good faith with the local city council, county council or commission, health department, state legislators, collective bargaining organization if applicable, and AG to find alternative outcomes allowing for continued availability of grocery establishment products and services at the closure location. This requirement may be satisfied by attending at least one community meeting convened by a neighborhood association or neighborhood organization representing the interests of neighborhood residents and one meeting with a collective bargaining organization, if applicable. Alternative outcomes may include identifying strategies or resources to allow the grocery establishment to remain open, assisting residents in organizing a cooperative, or identifying another grocery establishment capable of continuing similar products and services at or near the closure location. Reduced Notice. A grocery establishment may close for business having provided less than four months’ notice if the closure is due to a natural emergency or disaster or if, as of the time notice would have been required, the grocery establishment was actively seeking capital or business which, if obtained, would have enabled the grocery establishment to avoid or postpone the closure and the grocery establishment reasonably and in good faith believed that giving the required notice would have precluded obtaining the needed capital or business. A grocery establishment relying on reduced notice must give as much notice as practicable and, at the time notice is given, must articulate in writing the basis for having reduced the notice period. Enforcement. The AG may institute civil proceedings for injunctive and monetary relief, including civil penalties, for violations of the closure notice requirements and meeting obligations. A collective bargaining organization representing impacted employees, or the city attorney or county prosecuting attorney for the jurisdiction where the grocery establishment is located, may bring a civil action to enforce compliance and may seek injunctive or declaratory relief and a writ of mandamus. In an action brought by these parties, the court may award reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs to a prevailing plaintiff. Negative Use Restrictions. A negative use restriction that would prohibit a grocery establishment from operating at a location where such a use would otherwise be allowed is void and unenforceable.
