HB 2509 · EFSEC application review

Streamlines energy facility siting by limiting adjudication topics, granting SEPA categorical exemptions and deeming design-standard compliance to mitigate impacts, and mandating expedited processing for solar, wind, storage and transmission projects.

What the bill does (WA House Bill Analysis 2026 (Summary of Bill))

Energy Facility Site Evaluation Council Members . The EFSEC consists of the Chair and two additional members when considering an application for the following electric transmission facilities: an electrical transmission facility with a voltage of at least 115,000 volts that is located in more than one jurisdiction that has promulgated land use plans or zoning ordinances; or the construction, reconstruction, or enlargement of new or existing electrical transmission facilities that are: located in more than one county and in the Washington service area of more than one retail electric utility and of a nominal voltage of at least 500,000 volts alternating current or at least 300,000 volts direct current; or located in a national interest electric transmission corridor. The two additional members of EFSEC are appointed by the Governor and must be qualified by experience or training in pertinent matters pertaining to environmental regulations, energy infrastructure siting, Washington land use practices, tribal consultation practices, and permitting. At least one of the three members must have been admitted to practice law in Washington and engaged in the legal profession at the time of the person's appointment. Public Hearings . Applications for a specified electrical transmission facility are not required to hold an informational hearing or a land use and zoning ordinance hearing, but must be reviewed in an adjudicative hearing unless eligible for expedited processing. The EFSEC must limit the topics of the adjudication to the project's potential for specified impacts on the subject property and compliance with applicable design and development standards. Review of the design and development standards must be determined by EFSEC staff within 90 days of the submission of a complete application. Compliance with such design and development standards is deemed to mitigate or avoid adverse impacts to a nonsignificant level under SEPA. Categorical Exemptions . Any applicant operating a wind generation facility pursuant to an existing certification that applies to the EFSEC for an amendment to such certification for a repowering prior to July 4, 2026, must be categorically exempt from SEPA review if the EFSEC determines that the proposed project elements of the repowered facility will not be located outside of existing corridors or project boundaries approved by the council for the specified wind generation facility. Wind generation facilities that qualify for a categorical exemption are authorized to begin certain construction activities at the applicant's risk, but such activities are not a basis or indication for whether any required certification amendment will be approved by the EFSEC. Expedited Processing of Applications . The EFSEC must grant an applicant expedited processing of an application for solar, onshore wind, electricity storage, and transmission line facilities proposals if EFSEC finds that the: environmental impact of the proposed facility is not significant or will be mitigated to a nonsignificant level. Threshold determinations for any such proposal must be made within 90 days of a complete application; and applicant has completed a preapplication process with all federally recognized tribes potentially impacted. Upon request from a potential applicant, EFSEC must provide the applicant with information identifying the potentially affected federally recognized tribes.
