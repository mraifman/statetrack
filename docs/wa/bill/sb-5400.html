SB 5400 · Local news journalism

Creates a state grant program funding local civic journalists and imposes a 1.22% gross-income surcharge on large social media and search platforms starting 2027 to fund it.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The bill as referred to committee not considered. Summary of Bill (Proposed Substitute): Local News Sustainability Program. The Washington Local News Sustainability Program (Program) is established in the Department of Commerce (Commerce). Commerce must make grants to support the employment of news journalists covering civic affairs in underserved communities to deepen community understanding of complex community issues and policies. Eligible applicants must: be an eligible news journalism provider; and employ at least three news journalists in Washington per quarter in the four quarters prior to receiving a grant. If an eligible news journalism provider is not eligible for a grant solely due to employing only two news journalists, they may qualify if they have employed at least two news journalists per quarter in the eight quarters prior to receiving the grant. An eligible publisher of a newspaper may qualify for grants without meeting the journalism employment requirements. The dollar amount for each grant awarded must be proportional to the hours worked by journalists employed by the applicant. Ten percent of the total amount appropriated for grants must be used for grants to eligible news journalism providers who employ ten or fewer news journalists. The remainder of the grants must be distributed on a first-come, first-served basis. Grant recipients must provide progress and final reports to Commerce upon request. Commerce may consult with civic affairs leaders and local news industry leaders in developing the Program. It may also contract with a third-party administrator to administer the Program. Local Journalism Investment Surcharge and Account. Beginning April 1, 2027, a local journalism investment surcharge is imposed on covered platforms. The surcharge is equal to the gross income of the business subject to the service and other activities B&O tax multiplied by 1.22 percent. The maximum of combined surcharge paid by a covered platform is capped at $6 million annually. A covered platform is a for-profit business in this state who had a gross income of $5 million or more in the preceding calendar year and are a social media platform or search engine. The Local Journalism Investment Account is created. All revenues from the local journalism investment surcharge must be deposited into the account to fund the Program and the journalism fellowship program at the Washington State University. Definitions. An eligible news journalism provider is an eligible publisher or broadcaster. An eligible broadcaster is a person that: holds or operates under a broadcast license issued by the Federal Communications Commission; engages professionals to create, edit, and produce original content concerning local or regional matters of public interest through activities, including conducting interviews, observing current events, analyzing documents and other information, or fact checking through multiple news sources; updates their content on a weekly basis for each of the previous four quarters; uses an editorial process for error correction and clarification; and carries active media liability insurance and has been covered continuously over the previous calendar year. An eligible publisher is a person that publishes a qualifying publication. A qualifying publication is a newspaper, as defined in the B&O tax code, but includes publications issued once per month, or a website, application, or other digital service.
