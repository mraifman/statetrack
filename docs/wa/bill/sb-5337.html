What the bill does (WA Engrossed Second Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Beginning July 1, 2026, persons are prohibited from operating a memory care facility or memory care unit unless they are certified. "Memory care facilities" and "memory care units" are defined as assisted living facilities that market or represent themselves as providing memory care or specialized dementia care services. They may be an entire facility dedicated solely to serving residents with dementia or a unit within a larger facility that is dedicated to serving residents with dementia. A facility or unit may be considered a memory care facility or memory care unit if the assisted living facility has restricted egress that prevents residents with cognitive impairment from leaving the facility unaccompanied. "Memory care services" and "specialized dementia care services" are defined as services provided by an assisted living facility, in addition to domiciliary care services, that are responsive to the typical needs of an individual with dementia. "Dementia" is defined as the irreversible loss of cognitive or intellectual function such as thinking, remembering, and reasoning so severe that it interferes with an individual's daily functioning and everyday life. The certification requirement for memory care facilities and units does not prohibit assisted living facilities without restricted egress from providing care to residents with dementia as long as it does not represent itself as a memory care facility or that memory care is a specialty of the facility. The Department of Social and Health Services (Department) is responsible for the certification of memory care facilities and units. Requirements for initial certification include: having a valid, current license to operate the assisted living facility; not having a pattern of uncorrected or recurring significant enforcement actions related to resident harm; serious risk of harm; serious disregard for resident health, safety, or deterioration of quality of care; or civil fines based on determinations of moderate or serious severity; not having a stop placement or conditions on a license related to care or a license revocation or summary suspension; having permanent infrastructure that considers the specialized needs of residents with dementia; having a staffing plan that provides adequate staff to meet the assessed sleeping and waking patterns and needs of residents, including awake staff 24 hours per day and adequate to provide assistance with eating and drinking; providing a physical building structure with access to programming and daily activities; and having developed policies and procedures to respond to residents who may wander, outline actions to take when a resident is missing, and outline how the facility will seek and use consultative services to address resident behavioral challenges. Operating standards are established for memory care facilities and units to maintain their certification.
