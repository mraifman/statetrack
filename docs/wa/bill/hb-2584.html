What the bill does (WA House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

A sales and use tax exemption is established for the sale of qualifying farm equipment to an eligible farmer. This exemption expires on October 1, 2036. Certificate of Exemption . To obtain this exemption, the buyer must provide the seller with an exemption certificate as prescribed by the Department of Revenue (DOR). Buyers must keep records to verify eligibility for the exemption. Instead of an exemption certificate, a seller may record data as allowed under the SSUTA. The seller must retain a copy of the certificate or recorded data. Qualifying Farm Equipment . To qualify for this exemption, the farm equipment must have been acquired by an eligible farmer at a sales price of $10,000 or more. "Farm equipment" includes, but is not limited to, tractors, trailers, combines, tillage implements, balers, and other equipment, including attachments and accessories that are used in the planting, cultivating, irrigation, harvesting, and marketing of agricultural, horticultural, or livestock products, but does not include motor vehicles designed or intended for use upon public roadways or motorcycles. Eligible Farmers . To qualify for this exemption, either the gross sales or harvested value, or both, of agricultural products and bee pollination services of the eligible farmer, in combination with all affiliates of the eligible farmer, may not exceed the farm income threshold. An eligible farmer may not claim this exemption more than once, in total, each calendar year. "Eligible farmer" means a farmer who meets specified statutory requirements, including gross sales, harvested value, or estimated value of at least $10,000 in the immediately preceding tax year, or a farmer who did not engage in farming in the immediately preceding tax year because they are new to farming or newly returned to farming. "Farmer" means any person engaged in the business of growing, raising, or producing, upon the person's own lands or upon the lands in which the person has a present right of possession, any agricultural product to be sold, and the growing, raising, or producing of honey bee products for sale, or providing bee pollination services, by an eligible apiarist. "Farmer" does not include a person growing, raising, or producing such products for the person's own consumption; a person selling any animal or animal substance in connection with the person's business of operating a stockyard or a slaughter or packing house; or a person in respect to the business of taking, cultivating, or raising timber. "Farm income threshold" means $2 million in either gross sales or harvested value, or both, of agricultural products and bee pollination services in the preceding tax year. By December 2031, the DOR must adjust the farm income threshold based on the consumer price index as specified in the act. Tax Preference Performance Statement . The Legislature categorizes the tax preference in this act as one intended to provide tax relief for certain businesses or individuals. By January 1, 2035, the JLARC must evaluate the impact of these exemptions and changes in the number of small and medium-sized agricultural producers located in the State of Washington.
