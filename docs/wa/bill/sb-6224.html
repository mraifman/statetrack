What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The Children and Youth Behavioral Health Leadership Council (leadership council) is created through the Governor to address children and youth health and wellness issues and coordinate efforts to implement WTSP. The leadership council must include representation from representatives from state agencies involved in children and youth behavioral health or wellness activities identified by the Governor; representatives with lived experience caring for individuals with behavioral health issues experienced as a child or youth; and individuals representing a federally recognized Indian tribe or tribes whose traditional lands and territories included parts of Washington. Duties of the leadership council include reporting to the Governor on state agency practices and recommendations for changes in state policy that will help implement the WTSP; providing easily accessible and publicly available updates regarding the actions, decisions and recommendations of the leadership council; and notifying the Governor and appropriate committees of the Legislature when it believes that the executive coordination officer has accomplished the goals of that position and is no longer needed. The work group is allowed to advise and provide recommendations to the leadership council. The work group is required to use the WTSP to guide the priorities and efforts of the work group. The work group is extended for an additional two years from 2029 which is now set to expire in 2031. To the extent possible with existing resources, the Governor must support and facilitate coordination between various state agencies to follow the framework of the WTSP, and is allowed to use private funding sources to support the WTSP. The Governor must maintain an executive coordination officer for the children and youth system of care to: coordinate and monitor the implementation of the WTSP; build any infrastructure described in WTSP; coordinate private funding used for WTSP; and monitor in coordination with the Washington State Office of Equity any equity impacts of implementation of WTSP. All agencies are required to ensure that children and youth behavioral health activities and planning efforts align with WTSP and are implemented in an equitable manner.
