HB 2548 · Health care market standards

Expands health care material-change notice rules to hospital ownership, asset sales, and nonprofit-to-for-profit conversions, adds filing fees, and extends the Attorney General's review period.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute House Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Material Change Notice Requirements . The following additional transactions qualify as material changes that trigger the 60-day notice requirement for certain transactions involving health care market participants: transactions involving any entity or person that result in a change of majority ownership or control of a hospital, hospital system, or provider organization, including a merger, acquisition, or contracting affiliation; acquisitions, sales, or transfers of the majority of the assets of a hospital, hospital system, or provider organization including real property sale and leaseback transactions; or a conversion of a hospital, hospital system, or provider organization from a nonprofit corporation to a for-profit corporation or unincorporated entity. The written notice must include the name of any person or entity with a majority ownership, investment, or controlling interest of any party. A written notice is considered filed upon filing the written notice with payment of the required filing fee. The parties that filed a written notice must provide notice of the completion of the transaction to the Attorney General within 30 days after the transaction is completed. The Attorney General must make public on its website a quarterly notice of pending and completed material change transactions. The Attorney General must notify the Secretary of State when an entity involved in specified material change transactions is a nonprofit corporation. Attorney General Requests for Additional Information . If the Attorney General requests additional information from the parties to a material change transaction, the transaction may not proceed until 30 days after the parties have certified they are in substantial compliance with the request. Any subsequent request for additional information will not extend the 30-day waiting period for the parties to close the transaction. Filing Fees . Parties filing a written notice of a material change transaction must pay a filing fee. Only one filing fee is required per transaction. Filings fees are assessed based on the anticipated value of the transaction in Washington, as follows: for transactions of up to $1,000,000, a fee of $2,500; for transactions over $1,000,000 and up to $4,000,000, a fee of $7,500; for transactions over $4,000,000 and up to $10,000,000, a fee of $15,000; for transactions over $10,000,000 and up to $20,000,000, a fee of $20,000; for transactions over $20,000,000, a fee of $25,000; and for contracting affiliations involving no transfer of assets, a fee of $2,500. Parties to a contracting affiliation between a state-owned or state-operated entity and a public hospital district are not required to pay the filing fee. The Attorney General may index the filing fee amounts in accordance with the United States Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Price Index for medical care. All receipts from filing fees must be deposited in the Antitrust Revolving Fund. Defined Terms . Definitions are added for "affiliated with" and "control" or "controlling interest." The definition of "provider organization" is modified to note the list of qualifying entities in the definition is not exhaustive.
