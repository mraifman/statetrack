SB 6200 · Tenant cooling devices

Limits landlords' ability to prohibit or restrict tenants from installing portable cooling devices, with listed exceptions and a two-day notice requirement for window units.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

"Portable cooling device" is defined as an air conditioner, portable heat pump, or evaporative cooler, including a device mounted in a window or designed to sit on the floor, but does not include devices whose installation or use requires excessive alteration to the dwelling unit. "Window-mounted portable cooling device" is defined as a portable cooling device that is installed to sit in the window but does not include a saddle-mounted portable cooling device, a u-shaped portable cooling device, or a floor-mounted device that is connected to a window for air exchange or ventilation. "Saddle-mounted portable cooling device" is defined as a portable cooling device that hangs over the windowsill with interior and exterior components rather than sitting on top of it, such that the window view is not fully obstructed and the device will not fall when the window is opened, and includes u-shaped portable cooling devices. Tenant Use of Cooling Device under the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act. Under the RLTA, a landlord may not prohibit or restrict a tenant from installing a portable cooling device of any type unless: the dwelling already has a permanently installed and fully operational heat pump; or installation of the device would violate state or local building codes, state law, or federal law, violate the device manufacturer's written safety guidelines for the device, cause unreasonable damage to the premises or render the premises uninhabitable, or require an electrical supply to power the device that cannot be accommodated by the existing power service to the building, dwelling unit, or circuit. A landlord may also prohibit or restrict a tenant from installing a window-mounted portable cooling device if: the window is a necessary egress and the device would interfere with its use as egress; the device would interfere with the ability to lock the window; the device requires excessive use of brackets or other hardware that would damage or void the warranty of the window or frame, puncture the exterior wall of the building, or otherwise cause significant damage; the device cannot be secured in a way that prevents it from falling out of the window; or the landlord's insurance policy for the dwelling occupied by the tenant expressly restricts or prohibits the use of window-mounted portable cooling devices, this restriction or prohibition has been communicated to the tenant, and the landlord has provided written evidence of insurance policy restrictions or prohibitions to the tenant. As a condition of installing a portable cooling device, a landlord may require that the device be adequately drained to prevent damage to the dwelling unit or building. A landlord may also require that a window-mounted portable cooling device be subject to inspection or servicing by the landlord after installation. Nothing in the act shall preclude a landlord from charging for the rental of a portable cooling device rented by the tenant from the landlord, nor for any damages arising out of a tenant's use of a portable cooling device. Notice and Limitations under the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act. A tenant must provide at least two days' notice to a landlord prior to the installation of a window-mounted portable cooling device. A landlord must notify tenants in their lease of their rights, responsibilities and restrictions related to installation and operation of a portable cooling device.
