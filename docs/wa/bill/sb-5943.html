SB 5943 · Use of school impact fees

Temporarily lets school districts use impact fee money for safety upgrades and, in financial distress, for operations and maintenance, and repeals a 2009 timeline study directive.

What the bill does (WA Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

Until January 1, 2036, impact fees may be used for two new purposes in limited circumstances. First, if the ten-year timeline for using impact fees will conclude within four years, impact fees may be used to modernize school facilities to comply with state and federal laws regarding student safety, campus security, emergency response, and energy efficiency standards. Second, if a school district is in binding conditions or under enhanced financial oversight, the school district may use up to 25 percent of the money in its impact fee account for school district operations and maintenance. If the district is under enhanced financial oversight then the use of the impact fees for this purpose must be approved by the appointed special administrator. A statute from 2009, directing the Office of the Superintendent of Public Instruction to develop criteria for expanding the timeline of impact fees from six years to ten years is repealed. EFFECT OF CHANGES MADE BY EARLY LEARNING & K-12 EDUCATION COMMITTEE (First Substitute): Changes the authority to use impact fees for expanded uses expires January 1, 2036.
