SB 5975 · Safe cookware

Restricts intentionally added lead in aluminum and brass cookware from 2027, shifts lead limits, and adds cookware to Ecology's Safer Products program.

What the bill does (WA Engrossed Substitute Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

The definition of covered cookware is modified to exclude the electrical components of electronic cooking devices. Beginning January 1, 2027, no manufacturer may sell, offer for sale, distribute for sale, or distribute for use aluminum or brass cookware, utensils, or cookware components that contain lead which was intentionally added. The 90 parts per million lead level limit is applicable to cookware manufactured on or after January 1, 2026. The 10 parts per million lead content standard is eliminated. Ecology must develop and make available guidance to manufacturers regarding covered items, testing methods, compliance, and enforcement. Safer Products . Cookware containing lead is added as a priority product under the Safer Products program beginning with the June 1, 2029 review cycle. Ecology must adopt a lead content standard that is either ten parts per million of the lowest lead limit that is feasible. In evaluating cookware as a priority product, to the extent feasible, Ecology must consider the entire lifecycle of the product, including when surfaces become damaged, scratched, or heavily used. Ecology may also consider the end-of-life management and recyclability of the product. Ecology may order cookware manufacturers to submit data on lead levels in their products. To the extent practicable, Ecology must make the data publicly available. Manufacturers that fail to comply may be subject to per product penalties.
