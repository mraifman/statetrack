SB 5911 · DCYF/financial stability

Bars DCYF from using extended foster care youths' benefits to reimburse care costs and requires help securing Social Security benefits and protected accounts.

What the bill does (WA Substitute Senate Bill Report (Summary of Bill))

As of January 1, 2027, DCYF may not apply any benefits, payments, funds, or accrual paid to, or on behalf of, a person receiving EFC services from DCYF as reimbursement for the cost of their care. When a person receiving EFC services is not already receiving Supplemental Security Income and Retirement, Survivors, and Disability Insurance benefits, DCYF is to assess whether the person is eligible for such benefits. When a person is assessed to be eligible for Social Security benefits, DCYF shall: assist the person to become a payee for Social Security benefits and maintain eligibility for those benefits when DCYF is the representative payee; assist individuals, who are designated as payees for their benefits, establish a financial account appropriate for receiving those benefits—such as a Washington Achieving a Better Life Experience Account, a checking or savings account, or another form of electronic banking; make reasonable efforts to identify a suitable individual to serve as a person's authorized representative, if needed; and serve as the authorized representative if neither the person nor DCYF can identify a suitable individual until the individual or DCYF can identify a suitable individual to serve in place of DCYF. DCYF may contract with an external entity to administer and manage the accounts governed by this act on DCYF's behalf. When funds belonging to a person in DCYF's care exceeds $2,000, those funds may be deposited into a qualifying protected account, such as a Washington Achieving a Better Life Experience Program Account or a similar account, where the person is eligible for them.
